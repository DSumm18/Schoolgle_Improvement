# Rochdale Council School Improvement Assessor Screen Walkthrough

Date: Tuesday 19 May 2026  
Primary artifact: `index.html`  
Scope: School Improvement Assessor only. Use Rochdale to show the LA workspace, then PAYMAT to show the populated data example.

## Core Positioning

Schoolgle School Improvement Assessor gives a local authority or trust a fair, source-labelled view of school performance so advisers can prioritise support, ask better questions and move from data signals to improvement actions.

## Screen-Led Demo Flow

### 1. Main Dashboard

Screen: Rochdale Council dashboard.

Say:

> “This is the main Schoolgle workspace. For Rochdale, the top-level account is the local authority workspace. The schools and modules shown depend on the user’s permissions.”

Point out:

- organisation context in the sidebar;
- active app;
- tasks across modules;
- module navigation;
- Ed assistant.

### 2. Product Model

Screen: left sidebar / module list.

Say:

> “The parent layer is Rochdale Council. Under that sit connected schools. Each school can have its own modules, evidence and data, while the LA has the oversight view it is permitted to see.”

### 3. Open School Improvement

Screen: School Improvement expanded.

Say:

> “Today I’m focusing on the Assessor because that is the strongest thing to show. Ofsted Readiness is the workflow this can feed later, but the data engine is the important bit for this conversation.”

### 4. Rochdale School Improvement Assessor View

Screen: Local Authority School Improvement Assessor.

Say:

> “This is useful because it shows the real challenge: the system needs connected data. Rochdale has the workspace and the schools; once the relevant data captures are connected, this becomes the live assurance view.”

Use this line if it looks empty:

> “I’m going to use Pennine Academies Yorkshire as the populated example so you can see what this looks like when the data layers are connected.”

### 5. Explain Data Sources

Say:

> “There are three layers. First, published DfE data: historic, external, good for baseline and comparison. Second, school or trust assessment captures: current, useful, but self-reported. Third, pupil-level MIS or CTF evidence: the richest layer, because it explains cohorts, groups and journeys.”

Key phrase:

> “Schoolgle should always label source, date and scope. It must not silently blend those layers.”

### 6. PAYMAT Populated Example

Screen: PAYMAT School Improvement Assessor with data.

Say:

> “I’m switching to PAYMAT here because it has demo-ready data. This is what Rochdale would get once schools connect the same kinds of data captures.”

### 7. Data Capture Strip

Screen: data capture strip.

Talk through:

- mid-year spreadsheet = school/trust-provided current assessment data;
- DfE loaded = external baseline;
- school summary = extra current school-level capture;
- pupil data = MIS/CTF layer, unlocked only when connected.

Say:

> “This is where the credibility comes from. We know what the figure is, where it came from and how confident we should be.”

### 8. Key Findings

Screen: key findings cards.

Say:

> “This is what makes it different from a tracker. It turns the data into useful school improvement questions. These are triage signals, not accusations or inspection judgements.”

Examples to mention:

- strongest school;
- school needing attention;
- zero greater-depth writing flags;
- below-threshold combined outcome.

### 9. AI Question

If they ask “what AI are you using?”, say:

> “The School Improvement Assessor numbers are not a black-box AI judgement. The calculations are deterministic and source-labelled. AI is used where it adds value: narrative support, evidence analysis, document checks, drafting questions and suggested actions. For school/customer data, Schoolgle policy allows only approved provider families: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Meta Llama and Mistral. AI supports professional judgement; it does not replace it.”

If they ask about China or unapproved models:

> “No. Schoolgle’s product policy does not allow school/customer data to be sent to unapproved provider families.”

### 10. Challenges

Be honest:

- DfE data is historic.
- School-submitted captures are current but self-reported.
- Pupil counts can differ because datasets have different scopes.
- Pupil-level evidence needs safe school consent/connection.
- Missing data should become a visible question, not a hidden assumption.

Say:

> “That honesty is part of the product. It makes uncertainty visible instead of pretending everything lines up perfectly.”

### 11. Close

Ask for a pilot:

> “The sensible next step is a six-to-eight-week pilot with three to five Rochdale schools. Start with URNs and public data, then add assessment captures where schools agree. Then we review whether it saves adviser time and improves the quality of school improvement conversations.”

Final line:

> “Let us prove it with Rochdale’s schools, Rochdale’s questions and Rochdale’s data.”

## What Not To Say

- “The AI has judged the school.”
- “This is an Ofsted grade.”
- “The school is definitely weak.”
- “All pupil counts should match.”
- “We average Reading, Writing and Maths.”

## Safer Phrases

- “This source shows…”
- “The school-submitted view says…”
- “This raises a question for leaders…”
- “To make a stronger claim, we would want…”
- “This is a triage signal, not a judgement.”
