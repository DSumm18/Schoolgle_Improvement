Operations

Breakfast clubs — the national rollout starts April

Every state primary expected to offer a free breakfast club to all pupils. Three operational challenges need solving before April.

The funding

Phase 1 distributes in two tranches: fixed payment end of May 2026 (maintained) or early June (academies), plus a variable element based on pupil numbers. Early adopter schools have been testing since autumn 2025.

The practical challenges

Staffing. Who runs it? Existing staff on overtime? New hires? Volunteers? The grant is designed to cover costs, but the per-pupil rate may not stretch to paid staffing everywhere.

Space. Not every school has a hall free at 7:45am. If breakfast club clashes with before-school clubs, music lessons, or staff briefings, something moves.

Food. Procurement, allergen management, kitchen access. If your kitchen isn't operational before 8am, you need an alternative provision model.

Do this next
  • Read the Phase 1 guidance on GOV.UK. (15 mins)
  • Identify your three biggest operational challenges. (15 mins)
  • Talk to early adopter schools near you — they've already tested solutions. (30 mins)

Radar

PE and Sport Premium: new digital reporting

From July 2025, all schools must complete a digital expenditure reporting return for the PE and Sport Premium. Previously: publish a statement on your website. Now: the DfE gets granular data on how the money is spent.

Allocations: £16,000 + £10 per pupil (Years 1–6) for schools with 17+ eligible pupils. "We bought some footballs" won't cut it — you need expenditure linked to evidenced impact.

Do this next
  • Review PE Premium spending to date. Can you evidence impact? (30 mins)

The Schools White Paper: what to expect

Expected content: curriculum reform, single regulatory framework, QTS required for all state school teachers from September 2026, SEND inclusion bases timeline, RISE programme expansion.

Spring term data: making numbers work harder

The question leaders should ask out loud

Is the government asking schools to do more with less — or building towards a genuinely better-resourced system?

Free breakfast clubs for every primary. Inclusion bases for every secondary. SEND training for every teacher. The ambition has never been broader. But budgets have never been tighter, the workforce never more stretched, and the gap between policy ambition and operational reality never wider.

When the White Paper arrives, will it come with the funding, staffing, and timeline schools need? Or another set of expectations schools are left to figure out alone?
