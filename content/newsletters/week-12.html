The attendance data just dropped — and the gaps are widening

10 March 2026
Compliance

The DfE's latest attendance data reveals a persistent two-tier system

National absence rates have improved to 6.7%, but the gap between disadvantaged and non-disadvantaged pupils has widened to 3.4 percentage points — the largest since records began.

What the headline masks

While the national average has improved from its post-pandemic peak of 7.5% in 2022-23, the improvement is not evenly distributed. Schools in the most deprived quintile have seen only a 0.3pp improvement, compared to 1.2pp for the least deprived.

The persistent absence threshold (10%+) still captures 22.3% of all pupils — that's 1.8 million children missing the equivalent of one day a fortnight or more.

The SEND dimension

Pupils with an EHCP have an absence rate of 11.2%, nearly double the national average. For pupils on SEN Support, it's 9.1%. These figures have barely moved in three years, suggesting current interventions aren't reaching the pupils who need them most.

SEND

Why SEND attendance is the inspection question you're not ready for

Ofsted's new report cards will track attendance by subgroup. Schools that can't explain their SEND absence patterns will face difficult conversations.

Under the new inspection framework, inspectors are specifically looking at how schools support the attendance of vulnerable groups. The shift from single-word judgements to diagnostic report cards means schools need to evidence their approach, not just report their numbers.

Three questions inspectors will ask

  1. "How do you know which SEND pupils are persistently absent, and what have you done about it?" — Generic whole-school attendance strategies won't cut it. Inspectors want to see targeted, individual approaches.
  2. "How does your SEND provision address barriers to attendance?" — If a child with autism is persistently absent, what reasonable adjustments have you made to their school experience?
  3. "What does your attendance data look like disaggregated by SEND status?" — If you can't produce this quickly, that's a finding in itself.
"Schools that treat SEND absence as a welfare issue rather than a provision issue are missing the point. For many of these children, the absence is the feedback on your provision." — Senior HMI, regional conference, February 2026
Prepare now
  • Generate a SEND absence report disaggregated by primary need type
  • For your top 10 SEND persistent absentees, document what specific adjustments you've made
  • Brief your SENCo on the inspection questions above — can they answer confidently?
