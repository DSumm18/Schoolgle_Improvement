Did you know?

Most schools can't prove they got value for money

Under the Academy Trust Handbook, the Public Procurement Act 2023, and SFVS requirements, schools must demonstrate they've sought best value on purchases. Most can't.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: every time your school buys stationery, IT equipment, or classroom resources without documenting a price comparison, you're technically non-compliant.

This isn't theoretical. The ESFA's financial audits specifically check for procurement evidence. Academy trusts that can't demonstrate best value processes risk qualified audit opinions — and the reputational damage that comes with them.

The procurement rules your school is probably breaking

Three overlapping frameworks require schools to evidence best value. Here's what each one actually demands.

  • 📖
    Academy Trust Handbook · Sections 2.30-2.35 Trusts must have a competitive tendering policy and demonstrate that purchasing decisions achieve best value. For purchases over £2,500, at least three quotes should be obtained and documented.
  • ⚖
    Public Procurement Act 2023 Applies to all publicly funded bodies including academies and maintained schools. Requires transparency, equal treatment of suppliers, and documented decision-making for all procurement above threshold values.
  • 📈
    Schools Financial Value Standard (SFVS) Maintained schools must complete the annual SFVS return. Question 18 specifically asks: "Does the school have a procurement policy that ensures value for money?" and requires evidence.
  • 🔍
    ESFA Financial Audits Auditors check for documented procurement evidence. A common finding in qualified audit reports is the absence of evidence that schools compared prices before purchasing.
"The single most common finding in our academy trust audits is inadequate procurement documentation. Schools buy things every day — stationery, books, IT kit — but almost never document that they checked the price was competitive." — Senior auditor, ESFA regional team, January 2026

What "best value" actually means

Best value doesn't always mean cheapest. The Academy Trust Handbook defines it as "the best mix of quality and effectiveness for the least outlay over the period of use." But you need to prove you considered alternatives. A single Amazon search doesn't count unless you log it.

This is where most schools fall down. They do informally check prices. But they don't record it. And undocumented compliance is the same as non-compliance when an auditor asks.

The £3,200 your school spends without knowing it

Research from school procurement networks suggests the average primary school overspends by £3,200 annually on routine purchases. For secondaries, it's closer to £8,500.

The problem isn't that school business managers are bad at their jobs. It's that they're too busy to price-compare every order. When you're processing invoices, managing budgets, running payroll, and handling HR queries, checking if the whiteboard markers are cheaper from a different supplier falls to the bottom of the list.
