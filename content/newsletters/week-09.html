SEND

SEND — the pressure point nobody can ignore

EHCPs have risen from ~240,000 (2015) to 638,700 (Jan 2025). Demand has more than doubled. The system is paying for it in the most expensive way possible.

The funding still doesn't add up

Independent special school places average about £61,500 per year versus ~£24,000 in state special schools — largely because capacity constraints push councils into premium provision.

High-needs spending has been rising fast and is forecast to keep climbing. The IFS forecasts spending above £18bn by 2028–29. The LGA says 8 in 10 councils are at financial risk from SEND costs.

The SEND deficit "reset"

Government has said it will cover 90% of historic SEND deficits built up by March 2026. That helps councils' balance sheets — but it doesn't fix the engine causing the deficit in the first place.

IFS is explicit: without reform, councils were on course to build up a further ~£9bn of SEND-related debt across 2026–27 and 2027–28, plus a big in-year funding gap by 2028–29.

What's being briefed ahead of the White Paper

  • £3 billion for SEND places in mainstream schools (Dec 2025)
  • £200 million for SEND teacher training (Jan 2026)
  • £1 billion for "inclusion bases" in mainstream secondaries (Feb 2026)
  • £3.7 billion over 10 years for secondary facilities

The direction is clear: more SEND provision in mainstream, with specialist settings for the most complex needs.

The part that doesn't add up

The £200 million training programme covers every teacher in England, plus TAs, FE staff, and early years workers. That's well over a million people — roughly £200 per person. A two-day specialist SEND course typically costs £300–£500 per delegate. The budget doesn't stretch.

No announcement on improving TA pay — the people who actually deliver most SEND support in mainstream classrooms. TAs are often on term-time contracts earning less than equivalent retail roles. The inclusion model depends on them.

Do this next
  • Review your SEND register and provision map. When the White Paper drops, you'll need to know exactly where you stand. (1 hour)
  • Cost your current SEND provision — what are you spending from your own budget (not top-up) on SEN Support and EHCP delivery? (2 hours)
  • Brief your governors with a one-page SEND position summary: numbers, costs, gaps. (30 mins)

Radar

DfE: "Manage Your Education Estate" is rolling out

DfE is launching a new digital service in February 2026 to bring estates guidance, tools, funding and data into one place. If you're in a responsible body role, getting in early matters — early users shape what it becomes.

Do this next
  • Register for early access and provide feedback during the rollout window. (10 mins)

SEND transport: the silent budget-wrecker

Councils are warning that SEND transport costs could rise to £3.4bn a year by 2030/31 if nothing changes — up from roughly £2bn recently. Longer journeys are the symptom. Lack of local provision is the disease.

Do this next
  • Check what your LA is spending on transport for your pupils vs. the cost of the provision itself. If transport exceeds provision, that's a conversation for your next SEND review. (15 mins)

Cybersecurity policies need an AI update

Most school cybersecurity policies were written before generative AI existed. Students are using ChatGPT, Gemini, and dozens of other tools on school networks and devices. The filtering hasn't kept pace.

Do this next
  • Check whether your Acceptable Use Policy mentions AI tools by name. If it doesn't, it's out of date. (5 mins)
