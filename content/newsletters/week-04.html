£200 million for SEND training — the reality maths

The headline sounds transformative. The per-school allocation tells a different story.

On 16 January, the DfE announced a £200 million SEND teacher training programme. Adaptive teaching, assistive technologies, working with parents, changes to the SEND Code of Practice. The ambition covers early years, schools, and colleges.

Run the numbers

There are approximately 22,000 schools in England. £200 million ÷ 22,000 = roughly £9,000 per school. But the funding also covers early years settings and colleges — significantly increasing the number of institutions sharing the pot. Realistic per-school allocation: closer to £5,000–£6,000.

A single day of external SEND training costs £200–£350 per teacher including supply cover. £6,000 buys training for perhaps 20–30 staff across the programme's life — if none goes on resources or materials.

Source: DfE announcement, 16 January 2026; training cost estimates from school CPD providers

Welcome, but not transformative

Schools with existing SEND expertise will use it to top up. Schools where SEND training has been minimal will find it barely scratches the surface. A single EHCP assessment costs local authorities approximately £6,000, and the national backlog runs to tens of thousands.

SEND placements: where are pupils going?

The distribution of SEND placements varies enormously between local authorities. Some LAs place over 60% of EHCP pupils in mainstream settings. Others place less than 30%. Understanding your LA's pattern matters — it shapes your school's likely intake, resource needs, and the support your LA can offer.

Report cards: what the first wave reveals

Different grades across six areas — that's the point. But it creates new exposure.

The first batch of report cards from the new Ofsted framework is now public. Early patterns: schools receiving markedly different grades across the six evaluation areas. A school might get "Exceptional" for Leadership and Governance but "Needs attention" for Inclusion.

Under the old system, that school would have been "Good" — with the inclusion weakness hidden behind an aggregate judgement. For parents, this is more useful information. For schools, it's more exposure.

A "Needs attention" grade in any area is essentially a flag for deeper scrutiny at your next inspection. Address it proactively.

Do this next
  • If your school has received a report card, read it as a parent would. (15 mins)
  • If the story doesn't reflect reality, factual accuracy challenges can be submitted within the published window. (1 hour)

Falling school rolls: the crisis nobody's planning for

London sharpest, but it's a national trend. Smaller rolls mean less funding, fewer staff, fewer options.

The IFS Annual Report on Education Spending (December 2025) confirmed: pupil numbers falling rapidly across England. London: forecast decline of 3.8% in Year 7 places and 2.5% in reception between 2025/26 and 2029/30. Schools in North Yorkshire, Nottingham, and Hertfordshire already closing or merging.

The DfE's falling rolls fund for 2026–27: £141,890 per Middle Layer Super Output Area that sees a 10%+ pupil reduction. That's per area, not per school.

Source: IFS Annual Report on Education Spending, December 2025; Schools Week, January 2026

Do this next
  • Pull admissions data for the last five years. What's the trajectory? (30 mins)
  • If downward, start conversations now — governing body, trust, or LA. Mergers, federation, restructuring: all better discussed proactively. (ongoing)
