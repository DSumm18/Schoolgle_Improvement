INTENT

At Grove House Primary School, we have designed a broad and balanced curriculum that is ambitious for all learners, including those with SEND and those from disadvantaged backgrounds. Our curriculum reflects the context of our Bradford community — multicultural, richly diverse, and aspirational.

Our curriculum is built around three core principles:

1. KNOWLEDGE — Pupils acquire a body of powerful knowledge across all National Curriculum subjects, sequenced to build on prior learning and avoid cognitive overload. We have mapped knowledge progression from Reception to Year 6 in all subjects, drawing on Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction to ensure new content is connected to existing schema.

2. VOCABULARY — We have identified Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary for every unit across every year group. Vocabulary is pre-taught, displayed in classrooms, and revisited in retrieval activities. Reading across the curriculum is prioritised — every subject has a spine of high-quality texts.

3. CULTURAL CAPITAL — Our curriculum exposes pupils to a wide range of experiences they might not otherwise encounter: live theatre, museum visits, outdoor learning, author visits, and community projects. These are planned and purposeful, not tokenistic.

---

IMPLEMENTATION

SEQUENCING AND PROGRESSION
Each subject leader has produced a long-term curriculum map showing how knowledge builds from Year 1 to Year 6, with deliberate links to prior learning at each stage. For example, in History, pupils study the Vikings in Year 4 after building chronological understanding in Years 2 and 3, enabling deeper analysis of cause and consequence.

LESSON DESIGN
All lessons begin with a retrieval starter (low-stakes quizzing). Teachers use explicit instruction with modelling before guided and independent practice. This approach is rooted in evidence from the Education Endowment Foundation (EEF) Teaching and Learning Toolkit, particularly strategies rated as high-impact and low-cost: Metacognition, Feedback, and Explicit Instruction.

ADAPTIVE TEACHING
Class teachers are responsible for adapting the curriculum for all learners. Teaching assistants support within-class groups, guided by quality-first teaching, not withdrawal. Our curriculum is the same for all pupils; scaffolding is adjusted, not content removed.

IMPACT

We measure curriculum impact through:
- Termly pupil progress meetings (SLT and class teachers)
- End-of-unit assessments and teacher judgements
- Book scrutiny and learning walks each half-term
- National assessments: Phonics Screening Check (Year 1), SATs (Year 2 optional, Year 6)
- Pupil voice surveys conducted by subject leaders
- Governor deep-dive visits linked to curriculum priorities

In 2024, 83% of Year 6 pupils achieved expected standard in Reading, Writing and Maths combined, above the national average of 61%. Reading at greater depth was 38% (national: 24%).

Our curriculum is a living document. It is reviewed annually in consultation with staff, governors, and pupils.
