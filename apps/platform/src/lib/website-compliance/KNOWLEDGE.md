## 1. UK School Website Statutory Requirements (2025/2026)

All maintained schools and academies in England must publish specific information on their websites. This is not optional — Ofsted checks website compliance during inspection, and the DfE can direct schools to comply.

| Requirement                                                        | Legislation                                                                                                     | Update Frequency |

## 2. Academy vs Maintained School Differences

### Requirements That Apply ONLY to Academies

Academies have additional requirements under the **Academy Trust Handbook 2025** (ATH) and their funding agreements:

| Requirement                          | ATH Reference                                         | Details                                                 |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trust name and website link          | ATH 2025                                              | Must be clearly identifiable as part of the trust       |
| Articles of Association              | ATH 2025                                              | Must be published or linked                             |
| Memorandum of Association            | ATH 2025                                              | Must be published or linked                             |
| Funding Agreement                    | ATH 2025                                              | Must be published or linked                             |
| Whistleblowing Procedure             | ATH 2025 para 2.40                                    | Must be published — not required for maintained schools |
| Annual Report and Accounts (audited) | ATH 2025                                              | Due by 31 January following the year end                |
| Off-Payroll Arrangements Disclosure  | ATH 2025 para 2.30; HM Treasury Managing Public Money | Staff earning over 245/day equivalent                   |
| Related Party Transactions Register  | ATH 2025 para 5.50-5.52                               | Business interests and family relationships             |

### Requirements That Apply to BOTH

All other requirements listed in Section 1 apply to both maintained schools and academies. The School Information (England) Regulations 2008 (and its amendments) apply to all state-funded schools regardless of type.

### Key Practical Differences

- **Maintained schools** publish their admissions arrangements on the LA website and link to it. Their own school site only needs a link.
- **Academy schools** that are their own admissions authority must publish the full admission arrangements on their own (or trust) website.
- **Maintained schools** do not need to publish articles of association, funding agreements, or whistleblowing procedures on their website.
- **Academy trusts** must publish audited annual accounts; maintained schools' accounts are published by the local authority.

## 3. Church School Additional Requirements

### Voluntary Aided (VA) and Voluntary Controlled (VC) Schools

Church of England, Catholic, Methodist, and other faith schools have additional expectations beyond secular statutory requirements:

#### SIAMS (Statutory Inspection of Anglican and Methodist Schools)

- Schools in the Church of England and Methodist Church are inspected under the SIAMS framework as well as Ofsted.
- The school website should reflect the school's **Christian distinctiveness** and vision.
- The school's **Christian vision statement** should be prominently displayed.
- The most recent **SIAMS inspection report** should be published alongside the Ofsted report.

#### Additional Content Expected on Church School Websites

| Item                                 | Details                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Christian Vision / Mission Statement | Prominently displayed, showing how the school's Christian foundation shapes its work                                             |
| Collective Worship Policy            | How daily collective worship is organised; must be "wholly or mainly of a broadly Christian character" (Education Act 1996 s.70) |
| RE Curriculum (enhanced)             | VA schools set their own RE syllabus (often diocesan); VC schools follow the agreed syllabus but with denominational elements    |
| Diocesan Links                       | Link to the diocesan board of education website                                                                                  |
| Parish Links                         | Information about the relationship with the local parish church                                                                  |
| Foundation Governor information      | Details of foundation governors appointed by the diocese                                                                         |
| SIAMS Report                         | Copy of or link to the most recent SIAMS inspection report                                                                       |
| Ethos Statement / Christian values   | How Christian values are lived out in the school                                                                                 |

#### VA vs VC Differences

- **VA schools**: The governing body is the admissions authority and employer. More autonomy over RE and collective worship. Foundation governors form the majority.
- **VC schools**: The local authority is the admissions authority and employer. Follow the locally agreed RE syllabus. Foundation governors do not form the majority but are still present.

#### Catholic Schools

Catholic schools are inspected under **Section 48** (Religious Education in Catholic schools), not SIAMS. They should publish:

- Section 48 inspection report
- Catholic Life of the School statement
- Links to the diocese
- RE curriculum following the diocesan scheme (e.g., "Come and See")

## 4. Where Schools Typically Publish Policies

### The Trust-School Relationship for Policy Publication

Academy schools that are part of a **Multi-Academy Trust (MAT)** frequently publish policies on the **trust website** rather than (or as well as) the individual school website. This is a common and valid approach, but it creates challenges for website compliance checking.

#### Common Patterns

- **School website** links to trust website for policies (e.g., "Our policies can be found on the [trust name] website")
- **School website** hosts a policies page with PDF downloads that are trust-branded
- **Trust website** has a central policies page with all statutory policies for all schools
- Some policies are **school-level** (specific to that school) while others are **trust-level** (apply to all schools in the MAT)

#### Examples of Trust Website Domains

Schools in MATs often link to domains like:

- `paymat.org` (Pontefract Academies Trust)
- `dixonsca.com` (Dixons Academies)
- `outwood.com` (Outwood Grange Academies Trust)
- `deltaservices.org.uk` (Delta Academies Trust)
- `coopat.com` (Co-op Academies Trust)
- `oasisuk.org` (Oasis Community Learning)
- `arkaschools.org` (Ark Schools)
- `harrisfederation.org.uk` (Harris Federation)

#### Where to Look

When scanning a school website, policies may be found:

1. On the school's own `/policies` page as PDFs
2. Linked from the school site to the trust site
3. On Google Drive or Google Sites (some schools use these for document hosting)
4. On the trust's central policies page
5. Within PDF documents downloaded from any of the above

#### Implications for Compliance Checking

A school is considered compliant if the policy is **accessible from the school website** — even if it is hosted on the trust site. The key test is: "Can a parent, starting from the school's homepage, find this policy within a reasonable number of clicks?"

## 5. Trust-Level vs School-Level Content

### What Is Typically Published on the TRUST Website

For academy schools within a MAT, these items are commonly centralised on the trust website:

| Item                               | Rationale                                                      |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Articles of Association            | Single document for the whole trust                            |
| Memorandum of Association          | Single document for the whole trust                            |
| Funding Agreement                  | Trust-level agreement with DfE                                 |
| Annual Report and Accounts         | Trust-level financial statements                               |
| Whistleblowing Procedure           | Trust-wide procedure                                           |
| Complaints Procedure               | Often trust-wide with local contacts                           |
| Gender Pay Gap Report              | Trust-level (since the trust is the employer)                  |
| Off-Payroll Arrangements           | Trust-level financial disclosure                               |
| Related Party Transactions         | Trust-level governance document                                |
| Trust Board / Trustees information | Trust governance structure                                     |
| Scheme of Delegation               | Trust document showing what is delegated to individual schools |
| Data Protection / Privacy Policies | Trust is usually the data controller                           |

### What Should Be on the INDIVIDUAL School Website

Even within a MAT, these items must be specific to the individual school:

| Item                                        | Rationale                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Contact details (address, phone, email)     | School-specific                                              |
| Headteacher / Head of School name           | School-specific                                              |
| SENCO name and contact                      | School-specific                                              |
| SEN Information Report                      | School-specific (how THIS school meets SEN needs)            |
| Curriculum content by year group            | School-specific                                              |
| Phonics/reading schemes used                | School-specific                                              |
| Admission arrangements                      | School-specific (PAN, oversubscription criteria)             |
| Pupil Premium Strategy Statement            | School-specific (THIS school's PP numbers and spending)      |
| PE and Sport Premium report                 | School-specific                                              |
| KS2 / KS4 / KS5 results                     | School-specific                                              |
| Safeguarding Policy (or at least DSL names) | Must name THIS school's DSL and DDSL                         |
| Governance — Local Governing Body           | School-specific LGB members                                  |
| Ofsted report                               | School-specific                                              |
| Equality Objectives                         | Can be trust-wide but should include school-specific actions |

### The Critical Rule

**The school website must either contain the information directly OR provide a clear, working link to where it can be found.** A school cannot rely on parents knowing to go to the trust website separately.

| Online Safety Policy        | Safeguarding              | Policy             |
| Filtering and Monitoring    | Safeguarding              | Policy             |
| SEN Information Report      | Inclusion                 | SEND               |

### How Inspectors Use the Website

Before arriving at a school, Ofsted inspectors routinely check the school website. They look for:

1. **Safeguarding red flags** — Is the safeguarding policy current? Does it reference the latest KCSIE? Are DSL names published?
2. **Curriculum intent** — Does the curriculum page show clear intent, implementation, and impact thinking?
3. **Governance transparency** — Are governors named? Are interests declared? Is attendance recorded?
4. **SEND provision** — Is the SEN Information Report detailed and current? Does it show the graduated approach?
5. **Pupil Premium impact** — Is there evidence of effective use of PP funding with measurable impact?
6. **Data awareness** — Do published results show the school knows its data and context?

A well-maintained website gives inspectors confidence in the school's leadership and transparency before they even walk through the door.

## 7. Common Website Compliance Gaps

### Most Frequently Missing or Inadequate Items

Based on our scanning of school websites (including Grove House Primary and other test schools), these are the most commonly missing or problematic areas:

#### Frequently Missing Entirely

1. **Financial Benchmarking Link (FBIT)** — Many schools still link to the old Schools Financial Benchmarking site or have no link at all. The FBIT replaced the old service in 2024.
2. **Staff Pay Over 100k Disclosure** — Schools often omit this, even if the answer is "none."
3. **Website Accessibility Statement** — Despite being a legal requirement since 2018, many school websites have no accessibility statement at all.
4. **Filtering and Monitoring Standards** — A relatively new requirement (DfE standards published 2024), many schools have not yet published how they meet these standards.
5. **Equality Information (Annual)** — Schools often publish equality objectives but forget the separate annual equality information requirement.

#### Frequently Outdated

1. **Pupil Premium Strategy** — Many schools have last year's strategy but not the current year's.
2. **PE and Sport Premium** — Previous year's report still published with no current year update.
3. **Safeguarding Policy** — Still referencing a previous edition of KCSIE.
4. **KS2/KS4 Results** — Data from 2+ years ago with no current year results.

#### Frequently Inadequate (Present But Incomplete)

1. **SEN Information Report** — Published but missing several of the 13 mandatory Schedule 1 items (commonly missing: complaints procedure, transition arrangements, external body engagement).
2. **Governance Information** — Governors named but no terms of office, no attendance records, no register of interests.
3. **Curriculum Content** — Lists subjects but does not show content by year group.
4. **Admissions** — Arrangements published but PAN missing or oversubscription criteria unclear.

#### Frequently Poor Quality

1. **RSE Policy** — Policy exists but predates the September 2020 statutory requirement, showing no evidence of parent consultation.
2. **Behaviour Policy** — Published but lacks anti-bullying content or reasonable adjustments for SEND pupils.
3. **Complaints Procedure** — Single-stage process described instead of the required multi-stage approach (informal, formal, panel).

## 8. DfE Services Schools Must Link To

### Compare School Performance

- **URL**: `https://www.compare-school-performance.service.gov.uk/`
- **What it shows**: KS2, KS4, KS5 results; absence data; workforce data
- **Who must link**: All state-funded schools
- **Legislation**: School Information (England) Regulations 2008
- **Tip**: Schools should link to their specific school page, not just the homepage

### Financial Benchmarking and Insights Tool (FBIT)

- **URL**: `https://financial-benchmarking-and-insights-tool.education.gov.uk/`
- **What it shows**: School spending compared to similar schools, financial health indicators
- **Who must link**: All state-funded schools
- **Legislation**: School Information (England) (Amendment) Regulations 2025
- **Important**: This replaced the old "Schools Financial Benchmarking" service in 2024. Many schools still link to the old site — this needs updating.
- **Old URL (no longer valid)**: `https://schools-financial-benchmarking.service.gov.uk/`

### Local Offer

- **What it is**: The local authority's published information about services and provision for children and young people with SEN and disabilities
- **Who must link**: All schools with SEN pupils (effectively all schools)
- **Legislation**: Children and Families Act 2014; SEND Code of Practice 2015
- **Where to link**: The school's SEN Information Report must include a link to the local authority's Local Offer
- **Note**: Each LA has its own Local Offer website. The school should link to their specific LA's Local Offer page.

### Get Information About Schools (GIAS)

- **URL**: `https://get-information-schools.service.gov.uk/`
- **Who must link**: Not a statutory requirement to link, but schools should ensure their GIAS record is up to date as inspectors check it
- **What it contains**: Official school details — name, address, URN, school type, age range, headteacher name, governance type

## 9. Key Legislation References

### Primary Legislation

#### School Information (England) Regulations 2008 (SI 2008/3093)

- **As amended by**: SI 2012/1124, SI 2016/451, SI 2018/833, SI 2020/879, SI 2025/xxx
- **Scope**: The core regulation requiring schools to publish information on their websites
- **Key sections**: Reg 3 (school particulars), Reg 4 (admissions), Reg 5 (exam results), Reg 6 (curriculum), Reg 9 (curriculum content)
- **2020 amendment**: Added requirement to publish number of staff earning over 100k in 10k bandings
- **2025 amendment**: Updated financial benchmarking requirements (link to FBIT instead of old service); updated KS4 reporting metrics

#### Academy Trust Handbook 2025 (ATH)

- **Replaces**: Academies Financial Handbook (which became the Academy Trust Handbook in 2021)
- **Key paragraphs for website compliance**:
  - Para 1.49: Governance information publication
  - Para 2.29: Senior pay disclosure
  - Para 2.30: Off-payroll arrangements disclosure
  - Para 2.40: Whistleblowing procedure
  - Para 5.50-5.52: Related party transactions and register of interests
- **Scope**: Applies to all academy trusts (SATs and MATs)
- **Published by**: ESFA / DfE

#### Keeping Children Safe in Education 2025 (KCSIE)

- **Status**: Statutory guidance; schools MUST have regard to it
- **Key parts for website compliance**:
  - Part 1: What all staff should know (safeguarding policy must reference this)
  - Part 2: Management of safeguarding, including online safety, filtering and monitoring
  - Part 5: Child-on-child abuse
- **Updated annually**: Schools must ensure their safeguarding policy references the CURRENT edition. A policy citing "KCSIE 2023" in the 2025/26 academic year is a red flag.

#### Equality Act 2010

- **Section 149**: Public Sector Equality Duty — schools must have due regard to eliminating discrimination, advancing equality, and fostering good relations
- **Specific Duties Regulations 2011, reg 2**: Annual publication of equality information
- **Specific Duties Regulations 2017**: Equality objectives every 4 years
- **Schedule 10, para 3**: Accessibility Plan requirement (increasing disabled pupil participation in curriculum, improving physical environment, improving information accessibility)
- **Gender Pay Gap Information Regulations 2017**: Applies to trusts/schools with 250+ employees

#### Education (Guidance about Costs of School Uniforms) Act 2021

- Schools must have regard to DfE statutory guidance on the cost of school uniforms
- Must ensure uniform costs are reasonable
- Must consider second-hand provision
- Avoid single-supplier requirements for branded items where possible

#### Skills and Post-16 Education Act 2022

- Strengthened the "Baker Clause" (provider access legislation)
- Extended from Y8-13 to Y7-13
- Schools must publish a Provider Access Policy/Statement
- Must allow technical education and apprenticeship providers access to pupils

#### DfE Filtering and Monitoring Standards for Schools and Colleges 2024

- Published as a separate standards document (not part of KCSIE but referenced by it)
- Schools must meet these standards and should be able to demonstrate compliance
- Named person responsible for oversight of filtering and monitoring
- Regular reviews of filtering effectiveness
- Schools should publish how they meet these standards on their website

#### Children and Families Act 2014

- **Section 69**: Duty on maintained schools and academies to publish their SEN Information Report
- Read with SEN and Disability Regulations 2014 (SI 2014/1530), Schedule 1 (the 13 mandatory items)
- SEND Code of Practice 2015 provides statutory guidance on implementation

#### Education and Inspections Act 2006

- **Section 89**: Governing body must have a written behaviour policy (maintained schools)
- Must include anti-bullying measures

#### Education Act 1996

- **Sections 449-462**: Charging and remissions policy
- **Section 70**: Collective worship requirements (relevant to church school websites)

#### Education Act 2002

- **Section 29**: Complaints procedure requirement

#### School Admissions Code 2021

- Admission authorities must publish their admission arrangements
- For community and voluntary controlled schools: link to the LA page
- For foundation, voluntary aided, and academy schools: publish full arrangements on own/trust website
- Determined arrangements must be published by 15 March each year

## 10. Practical Advice for Schools

### What To Do If You Are Non-Compliant

1. **Do not panic.** Website compliance gaps are extremely common. Most schools have at least a few items missing or outdated.
2. **Prioritise by risk.** Not all gaps carry equal risk. Focus on safeguarding and statutory requirements first.
3. **Document your plan.** If an inspector finds gaps, having a documented action plan shows leadership awareness.
4. **Delegate clearly.** Assign a named person (often the school business manager, office manager, or deputy head) as the "website compliance lead."

### Priority Order for Fixing Gaps

**Tier 1 — Fix Immediately (Safeguarding and Legal Risk)**

1. Safeguarding / Child Protection Policy (must reference current KCSIE)
2. DSL and DDSL names and contact details
3. Online Safety Policy
4. Filtering and Monitoring statement
5. Behaviour Policy with anti-bullying measures

**Tier 2 — Fix Within 2 Weeks (Core Statutory)** 6. SEN Information Report (complete, covering all 13 items) 7. Pupil Premium Strategy Statement (current year) 8. Admissions arrangements (current year) 9. Curriculum content by year group 10. Governance information (names, interests, attendance)

**Tier 3 — Fix Within 1 Month (Important Statutory)** 11. KS2/KS4/KS5 results (most recent data) 12. Link to Compare School Performance 13. Link to Financial Benchmarking and Insights Tool (FBIT) 14. Pay over 100k disclosure 15. Equality Objectives and annual equality information 16. Complaints Procedure 17. Charging and Remissions Policy 18. RSE/RSHE Policy 19. School Uniform Policy (with cost considerations) 20. PE and Sport Premium report (primary)

**Tier 4 — Fix Within a Term (Compliance and Quality)** 21. Website Accessibility Statement (WCAG 2.2 AA) 22. Ofsted report link 23. Careers programme (secondary) 24. Provider Access Policy / Baker Clause (secondary) 25. Academy trust information (academies only) 26. Whistleblowing procedure (academies only) 27. Annual accounts (academies only) 28. Gender Pay Gap report (if 250+ employees)

### How Often to Review

| Review Frequency                | What to Check                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Termly** (minimum)            | All policies are current edition; no broken links; DSL names correct after any staff changes                            |
| **Start of each academic year** | Safeguarding policy updated for new KCSIE; curriculum pages updated; governance details refreshed; results data updated |
| **By 31 December**              | Pupil Premium Strategy published for current year                                                                       |
| **By 31 January**               | Academy annual accounts published                                                                                       |
| **By 28 February**              | Appeals timetable published; admission arrangements determined                                                          |
| **By 15 March**                 | Admission arrangements published for following year                                                                     |
| **By 31 July**                  | PE and Sport Premium report published                                                                                   |
| **Annually**                    | Full website compliance audit against all statutory requirements                                                        |

### Quick Wins for Improving Compliance Score

1. **Add a "Statutory Information" or "Key Information" page** — a single page that links to all statutory documents makes it easy for parents AND inspectors to find everything.
2. **Date all policies** — include "Approved: [date]", "Review date: [date]" on every policy document.
3. **Add a "none" statement for pay over 100k** — even if no one earns over 100k, state this explicitly to show you have considered the requirement.
4. **Update your FBIT link** — if you still link to the old Schools Financial Benchmarking site, update it to the new FBIT URL.
5. **Add an Accessibility Statement** — even a basic one is better than none. The GDS provides a template at https://www.gov.uk/guidance/model-accessibility-statement.
6. **Name your SENCO on the website** — not just in the SEN Information Report PDF.
7. **Publish curriculum content per year group** — even a simple table or document per year group meets the requirement. It does not need to be elaborate.

### What Happens If You Do Not Comply

- **Ofsted** will note website non-compliance in their inspection. While it alone will not change a grading, it contributes to the overall picture of leadership and management effectiveness.
- **The DfE** can issue a statutory direction to governing bodies or academy trusts requiring them to comply with the School Information Regulations.
- **Parents and stakeholders** may complain to the DfE or Ofsted about missing information, which can trigger a monitoring visit.
- **Reputationally**, an incomplete or outdated website signals poor attention to detail and can undermine parent and community confidence.

#### Website Compliance Checker (£50/year)

A standalone app that any school can use immediately. It:

8. **Reports**: found / not_found / needs_checking per requirement, with document summaries, quality scores, and expandable criteria breakdowns

The quick scan takes 30–90 seconds (longer for academies due to trust website crawling) and requires no browser, no AI, and no per-scan cost.
