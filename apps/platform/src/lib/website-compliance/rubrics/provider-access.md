# Provider Access Policy Statement

## Legislation

- Technical and Further Education Act 2017, Section 42B (the "Baker Clause")
- Careers Guidance and Access for Education and Training Providers
  (Statutory Guidance) January 2023
- Education Act 1997, Section 42A (duty to provide careers guidance)
- School Information (England) Regulations 2008 (as amended)

## Current References

Must comply with the strengthened Baker Clause requirements that came
into force in January 2023. The January 2023 statutory guidance
significantly expanded the requirements, introducing mandatory minimum
encounters and specific access entitlements by key stage. Any provider
access statement that does not reflect the 2023 changes is out of date.

References to the "Gatsby Benchmarks" (Good Career Guidance, Gatsby
Foundation 2014) should be present as these underpin the statutory
careers guidance framework.

## Review Frequency

Annually. The statement must be reviewed each academic year and updated
to reflect any changes in arrangements. The governing body should
approve the statement. It must be published before the start of each
academic year (by September).

## Required Content

1. A named person (with contact details) to whom providers should
   address access requests — this must be a specific individual, not
   just "the careers team" or "the school office"
2. A description of the circumstances in which providers will be given
   access to pupils — including careers fairs, assemblies, workshops,
   taster sessions, curriculum time
3. How the school manages provider access requests, including the
   process for making and granting requests, and reasonable grounds
   for declining
4. The minimum number of provider encounters by key stage, as required
   by the January 2023 statutory guidance:
   - Key Stage 3 (Years 7-9): at least 2 encounters with approved
     technical education providers or apprenticeship providers
   - Key Stage 4 (Years 10-11): at least 2 encounters with approved
     technical education providers or apprenticeship providers
   - Key Stage 5 (Years 12-13): at least 2 encounters with approved
     higher education providers and 2 with approved technical education
     or apprenticeship providers
5. A description of premises and facilities that will be made available
   for providers
6. How the school will ensure that encounters are meaningful — not
   token or superficial

## Quality Indicators

- Named Careers Leader with contact details (not just the provider
  access contact)
- Evidence of a full careers programme linked to the Gatsby Benchmarks
- Calendar or schedule of upcoming provider encounters
- List of providers who have accessed the school in the current/previous
  academic year
- Links to the school's broader careers strategy or programme
- Information about how the school evaluates the quality and impact
  of provider encounters
- Reference to Compass+ (the Gatsby Benchmark assessment tool)
- Statement about how the school ensures impartial careers information
- Details of how SEND pupils and disadvantaged pupils are supported
  in accessing providers
- Information about virtual/remote encounter options

## Red Flags

- No provider access statement on the website (statutory breach)
- Statement does not include the mandatory minimum encounters
- No named contact person for providers
- Statement predates January 2023 and does not reflect strengthened
  requirements
- School appears to restrict access to certain types of provider
  (e.g. only university providers, no apprenticeship/technical providers)
- Statement suggests providers can only access pupils outside
  curriculum time (the 2023 guidance allows curriculum time access)
- No process described for how providers can request access
- Statement is clearly a generic template with no school-specific content

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Named contact person with direct contact details
- Minimum encounters by key stage stated (matching 2023 requirements)
- Process for provider access requests clearly described
- Circumstances for access described with specific examples
- Premises/facilities information included
- Current academic year content
- Reflects January 2023 statutory guidance

### Partial (40-79)

- Statement exists but does not include all mandatory minimum encounters
- Named contact but no direct contact details
- Generic description of circumstances without specifics
- Pre-dates 2023 strengthened requirements but contains some relevant
  information
- Process for requests vague or incomplete
- No mention of premises/facilities

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No provider access statement on the website
- Statement is pre-2023 with no update
- No named contact person
- No minimum encounters stated
- Statement actively discourages or restricts provider access
- Generic template with no school-specific information

## Notes

- This requirement applies to SECONDARY SCHOOLS ONLY (including
  schools with sixth forms, UTC, studio schools). Primary schools
  are not required to publish a provider access statement.
- The January 2023 statutory guidance significantly strengthened the
  Baker Clause. Schools that published a statement before this date
  and have not updated it are non-compliant.
- Schools cannot refuse access on unreasonable grounds. The statutory
  guidance provides limited grounds for refusal (e.g. safeguarding
  concerns about specific individuals).
- The Careers & Enterprise Company provides template statements, but
  schools should customise these with school-specific details.
- Ofsted inspectors may check the provider access statement during
  inspection and ask about how the school meets its careers duties.
- In MATs, each secondary school must publish its own statement — a
  trust-wide statement is not sufficient unless it includes
  school-specific contact details and arrangements.
- Free schools and academies have the same obligations as maintained
  secondary schools under the Baker Clause.
