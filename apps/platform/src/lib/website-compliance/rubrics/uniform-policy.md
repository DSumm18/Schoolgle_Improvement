# Uniform Policy

## Legislation

- Education (Guidance about Costs of School Uniforms) Act 2021
- DfE "Cost of School Uniforms" statutory guidance (November 2021)
- School Information (England) Regulations 2008
- Equality Act 2010 (religious and cultural dress, gender)
- Human Rights Act 1998, Article 9 (freedom of religion)

## Current References

Must comply with the statutory guidance issued under the Education (Guidance
about Costs of School Uniforms) Act 2021, which came into force in autumn 2022.
Schools should demonstrate awareness of the cost-of-living considerations
introduced by this guidance. Any sole-supplier arrangements that existed before
the guidance must have been reviewed.

## Review Frequency

Annually, approved by governing body. The 2021 Act requires governing bodies to
have regard to the statutory guidance when developing and implementing uniform
policies. Must show a review date or academic year.

## Required Content

1. Full list of required uniform items for everyday wear (with clear indication of what is compulsory vs optional)
2. PE kit and sportswear requirements
3. Where uniform can be purchased — must offer multiple supplier options (high street or supermarket alternatives for generic items)
4. Branded items — must be kept to a minimum; school should explain why any branded items are necessary
5. Cost consideration statement — evidence that the governing body has given highest priority to cost and value for money
6. Second-hand uniform provision — how to access (shop, swap, donation scheme)
7. Supplier competition — if using a single supplier for branded items, evidence that the arrangement is regularly reviewed and tendered
8. Appearance standards — hair, jewellery, make-up expectations (must be non-discriminatory)
9. Religious and cultural dress — how the school accommodates religious head coverings, modesty requirements, cultural items
10. Transition arrangements — how families are supported when changing to new uniform requirements
11. Financial hardship support — how families experiencing difficulty can seek help, and who to contact confidentially
12. Seasonal variations — summer and winter uniform options if applicable

## Quality Indicators

- Written in plain, parent-friendly language with specific item names and colours
- Photos or illustrations of acceptable uniform
- Direct links to suppliers including online options
- Price guide or approximate costs for a full uniform set
- Named contact for hardship support (not just a generic "contact the office")
- Demonstrates genuine engagement with the cost agenda (not just a policy statement)
- Clear about which items MUST be branded and which can be generic alternatives
- Second-hand uniform actively promoted (not just mentioned)
- Gender-neutral options available or uniform list is not gendered
- Published as an accessible webpage (not just a PDF download)

## Red Flags

- No uniform policy published on the website
- Sole supplier arrangement with no justification or review process
- Excessive number of branded items (more than 3-4 branded items is a concern)
- No second-hand uniform provision mentioned
- No mention of cost considerations or the 2021 Act
- Discriminatory appearance standards (e.g. hairstyle rules that disproportionately affect certain ethnic groups)
- No accommodation for religious or cultural dress
- Uniform requirements that are excessively expensive with no financial support
- Policy predates November 2021 statutory guidance with no evidence of review
- Gender-specific uniform with no option for alternatives

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Policy published and accessible on website
- Full uniform list with clear compulsory vs optional distinction
- Multiple supplier options offered (not sole supplier, or sole supplier justified and reviewed)
- Second-hand uniform provision described
- Cost consideration evident in policy
- Religious and cultural accommodations addressed
- Review date within last 14 months

### Partial (40-79)

- Policy exists but missing cost consideration elements
- Sole supplier with no review or justification mentioned
- No second-hand provision described
- Missing religious/cultural accommodations
- Outdated (predates 2021 Act) but otherwise adequate

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No uniform policy found on website
- Policy exists but with sole supplier and no alternatives
- No evidence of compliance with 2021 Act requirements
- Brief list only with no policy substance

## Notes

- The Education (Guidance about Costs of School Uniforms) Act 2021 was a
  significant change. Schools had until autumn 2022 to comply. The statutory
  guidance places a clear duty on governing bodies to give "highest priority
  to cost and value for money" when setting uniform policy.
- The sole-supplier issue is the most common area of non-compliance. Schools
  can use a preferred supplier for branded items but must ensure the arrangement
  is competitive and regularly reviewed.
- Ofsted does not directly inspect uniform policy, but complaints about uniform
  cost can trigger scrutiny and the DfE can issue a direction if a school is
  not complying with the statutory guidance.
- Some schools publish uniform information on their admissions or parents page
  rather than as a standalone policy. This is acceptable if the required content
  is present.
- Academy trusts may have a trust-wide uniform policy with school-specific
  variations. Both levels should be accessible.
