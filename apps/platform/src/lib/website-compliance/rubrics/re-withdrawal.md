# Right to Withdraw from RE

## Legislation

- Education Act 1996, Section 71 (right to withdraw from RE)
- Education Act 1996, Sections 376-377 (right to withdraw from collective worship)
- School Information (England) Regulations 2008, Schedule 4
- School Standards and Framework Act 1998, Section 69 (RE in schools)
- Human Rights Act 1998, Article 9 (freedom of religion) and Protocol 1,
  Article 2 (right to education respecting parents' convictions)

## Current References

No annually changing reference. The right to withdraw is established in
primary legislation (Education Act 1996) and does not change year to year.
However, the school should ensure its statement reflects current practice
and is not obviously outdated (e.g. referencing a headteacher who has left).

Schools should be aware that DfE and Ofsted distinguish between Religious
Education (RE) and collective worship — parents have separate rights for each.

## Review Frequency

No statutory review cycle, but should be checked annually as part of the
school's website compliance review. The statement should be accurate and
reflect the school's current arrangements.

## Required Content

1. Clear statement that parents have the right to withdraw their child
   from Religious Education (RE), either wholly or partly
2. Explanation of how parents can request withdrawal — who to contact
   (headteacher or named person) and how (letter, email, meeting)
3. What happens to the child during RE lessons if withdrawn — the school's
   arrangements for supervision and alternative provision
4. Separate statement about the right to withdraw from collective worship,
   making clear this is a distinct right from RE withdrawal
5. Confirmation that there is no requirement for parents to give reasons
   for withdrawal (though schools may invite discussion)
6. For sixth-form students (aged 18+): statement that the student themselves
   can choose to withdraw, not just the parent

## Quality Indicators

- Distinction between RE and collective worship is clearly explained
- Tone is neutral and welcoming — not discouraging withdrawal
- Explanation of what RE covers at the school (locally agreed syllabus or
  academy's chosen approach) so parents can make an informed decision
- Mention that the school is happy to discuss the RE curriculum with parents
  before a formal withdrawal request
- Information about the RE curriculum alongside the withdrawal statement
- Named contact person (not just "the school")
- Available in accessible formats or languages where the school serves
  communities who may need this
- For Church of England or faith schools: clear statement about how RE
  is taught in the context of the school's religious character, and whether
  withdrawal applies to all aspects or only denominational content

## Red Flags

- No mention of the right to withdraw from RE anywhere on the website
- Website actively discourages withdrawal or implies negative consequences
- No distinction between RE and collective worship withdrawal rights
- No information about what happens to a withdrawn child during RE
- Statement suggests parents must give reasons or gain approval
- Withdrawal right mentioned only in an inaccessible PDF buried deep
  in the website
- Statement says withdrawal is "not available" or "not applicable" — this
  is incorrect; the right exists in all maintained schools
- For academies with no religious character: incorrectly states RE is not
  taught (academies must provide RE unless explicitly exempted in funding
  agreement)

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Clear statement of parents' right to withdraw from RE
- Process for requesting withdrawal is described (who and how to contact)
- Alternative arrangements during RE lessons are stated
- Distinction between RE and collective worship withdrawal is made
- Information is easy to find on the website
- Tone is neutral and informative

### Partial (40-79)

- Right to withdraw is mentioned but one or more of:
  - No process described for how to request withdrawal
  - No mention of what happens to the child during RE
  - RE and collective worship withdrawal not distinguished
  - Information is buried in a lengthy policy document
  - Statement exists but is vague or incomplete

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No mention of the right to withdraw from RE on the website
- Right to withdraw is explicitly denied or discouraged
- Only a passing reference with no practical information
- Page or section exists but contains no actual content

## Notes

- This is a commonly overlooked website requirement. Many schools publish
  their RE curriculum but forget to include the withdrawal right.
- Church of England and other faith schools must still allow withdrawal
  from RE, though they may explain that RE is central to the school's
  ethos and character. They cannot refuse withdrawal.
- In practice, very few parents exercise this right, but the information
  must still be published.
- Some schools include this information within their RE policy, their
  curriculum pages, or a dedicated page. Any location is acceptable as
  long as it is reasonably findable.
- The right to withdraw applies to RE specifically, not to learning about
  different cultures and religions as part of PSHE, geography, or history.
  Schools may wish to clarify this distinction.
- Collective worship withdrawal is a separate right under different
  legislation (School Standards and Framework Act 1998, s.71). Schools
  should ideally address both but must at minimum cover RE withdrawal.
- SACRE (Standing Advisory Council on Religious Education) guidance in
  some local authorities provides template wording that schools can adopt.
