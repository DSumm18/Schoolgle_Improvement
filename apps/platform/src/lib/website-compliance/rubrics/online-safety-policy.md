# Online Safety Policy

## Legislation

- Keeping Children Safe in Education 2025, Part 2 (Management of Safeguarding — online safety)
- DfE "Meeting Digital and Technology Standards in Schools and Colleges" (Filtering and Monitoring Standards, March 2023, updated 2024)
- UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 (pupil data online)
- Voyeurism (Offences) Act 2019 (upskirting)
- Online Safety Act 2023
- Education Act 2011 (searching and confiscation powers)

## Current References

Must reference KCSIE 2025 Part 2 for online safety responsibilities. Must
demonstrate compliance with the DfE Filtering and Monitoring Standards (2023).
Should reference the Online Safety Act 2023 where relevant. References to
specific filtering/monitoring providers should be current. Any reference to
KCSIE 2024 or earlier without also referencing 2025 means the policy has not
been updated.

## Review Frequency

Annually, approved by governing body. The DfE Filtering and Monitoring Standards
require regular review of filtering and monitoring arrangements. Must show a
review date or academic year. Should be reviewed whenever there is a significant
online safety incident or change in technology provision.

## Required Content

1. School's overall approach to online safety — vision, principles, how it is embedded across the curriculum
2. Roles and responsibilities — named DSL and online safety lead, governor with oversight, staff responsibilities, pupil responsibilities
3. Filtering arrangements — what filtering system is used, what categories are blocked, how filtering is checked and tested, who manages it
4. Monitoring arrangements — what monitoring system is used, how alerts are managed, who reviews them, how concerns are escalated
5. Annual filtering and monitoring review — statement that the school carries out an annual review of its filtering and monitoring provision as required by DfE standards
6. Staff training — how staff are trained on online safety, frequency, who provides it
7. Teaching online safety — how online safety is taught to pupils across the curriculum (not just in computing lessons)
8. Reporting procedures — how pupils, staff, and parents report online safety concerns
9. Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) — summary or link to full AUP for staff and pupils covering use of school devices and networks
10. Mobile phones and personal devices — policy on use in school (cross-reference to behaviour policy)
11. Social media guidance — staff use of social media, professional boundaries, school social media accounts
12. Responding to online safety incidents — process for managing incidents including preservation of evidence, referral routes (CEOP, police, IWF)
13. Cyber-bullying — definition, prevention, and response (cross-reference to anti-bullying/behaviour policy)
14. Sharing of nudes and semi-nudes (sexting) — how the school responds, referral to DSL, UKCIS guidance reference
15. Remote and home learning — online safety measures for virtual lessons and platforms

## Quality Indicators

- Written in accessible language for parents as well as staff
- Named online safety lead with contact details
- Specific filtering and monitoring products named (not just "we have filtering")
- Evidence of annual filtering/monitoring review (date of last review)
- Links to useful resources for parents (CEOP, Internet Matters, NSPCC, Childnet)
- Pupil voice — evidence that pupils contributed to online safety rules
- Cross-references to safeguarding, behaviour, RSE, and acceptable use policies
- Staff AUP published or summarised
- Advice for parents on supporting online safety at home
- Published as an accessible webpage, not just a PDF buried in a policy section

## Red Flags

- No online safety policy found on the website
- No mention of filtering and monitoring arrangements
- Does not reference KCSIE 2025 or DfE Filtering and Monitoring Standards
- No named online safety lead or DSL reference
- No procedure for reporting online safety concerns
- No mention of staff training on online safety
- Policy does not address sharing of nudes/semi-nudes
- Filtering and monitoring described vaguely with no evidence of regular review
- No acceptable use policy or AUP referenced
- Policy predates DfE Filtering and Monitoring Standards (March 2023)
- No mention of social media guidance for staff

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Policy published and accessible on website
- Filtering and monitoring arrangements clearly described
- References KCSIE 2025 and DfE Filtering and Monitoring Standards
- Named DSL and online safety lead
- Reporting procedures for incidents clearly stated
- Staff training described
- Covers teaching of online safety to pupils
- Review date within last 14 months

### Partial (40-79)

- Policy exists but filtering/monitoring detail is vague
- Does not reference DfE Filtering and Monitoring Standards
- Missing sharing of nudes/semi-nudes procedures
- No staff training information
- Generic template not customised to school
- Missing 3-4 required elements
- References KCSIE 2024 but not 2025

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No online safety policy found on website
- Policy exists but is a brief paragraph with no substance
- No reference to filtering, monitoring, or reporting
- Policy is significantly outdated (predates 2023 standards)

## Notes

- The DfE Filtering and Monitoring Standards (March 2023) were a significant
  step change. Schools must now demonstrate that they actively review and test
  their filtering and monitoring, not just have it in place. Ofsted will check
  this during inspection.
- KCSIE 2025 Part 2 is the primary reference point for online safety governance.
  It requires schools to have "appropriate filtering and monitoring systems in
  place" and to review them regularly.
- Many schools have a separate Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) for staff and pupils
  alongside the main online safety policy. This is acceptable but the AUP should
  be referenced or linked.
- The UKCIS "Sharing nudes and semi-nudes" guidance (updated 2020) should inform
  the school's approach. Schools should not criminalise children but must refer
  to the DSL and follow KCSIE procedures.
- Online safety should be taught across the curriculum, not just in computing
  lessons. KCSIE 2025 emphasises that it should be embedded in PSHE, RSE,
  and other subjects.
- Schools may have their IT managed by a trust, LA, or external provider.
  Regardless of who manages the technical infrastructure, the school/governing
  body retains responsibility for ensuring standards are met.
