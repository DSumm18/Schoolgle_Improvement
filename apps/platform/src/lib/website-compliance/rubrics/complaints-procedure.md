# Complaints Procedure

## Legislation

- Education Act 2002, Section 29
- School Information (England) Regulations 2008, Schedule 4
- Education (Independent School Standards) Regulations 2014 (academies/free schools)
- DfE "Best Practice Advice for School Complaints Procedures" (2019, updated 2022)

## Current References

Must reference the school's own complaints procedure document. Academies must
reference the Education (Independent School Standards) Regulations 2014.
Maintained schools must reference the local authority model if adopted, or their
own governing body approved procedure. References to the ESFA complaints process
should be current (gov.uk link, not legacy EFA references).

## Review Frequency

Annually, approved by governing body. Must show a review date or academic year.
Academies are required to have this as a condition of their funding agreement.

## Required Content

1. Definition of a complaint vs a concern (and how concerns are handled informally)
2. Stage 1 — Informal resolution: who to approach first (class teacher, head of year), expected timescale for acknowledgement and response
3. Stage 2 — Formal written complaint to headteacher: how to submit, acknowledgement timescale (typically 5 school days), investigation process, written outcome timescale (typically 15 school days)
4. Stage 3 — Escalation to governing body / complaints committee: how to request a panel hearing, composition of panel (minimum 3 governors not previously involved), timescale for convening (typically 20 school days)
5. Panel hearing procedure: right to attend and be accompanied, how evidence is presented, written outcome and notification timescale
6. Final stage — Escalation to external bodies: ESFA (academies) or Secretary of State, Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman (maintained schools), Ofsted (if school not following statutory duties)
7. Named contact or role for complaints (e.g. school business manager, clerk to governors)
8. How to submit a complaint (written, email, or form — should be accessible)
9. Confidentiality and record-keeping statement
10. How serial or vexatious complaints are handled
11. Statement that complainants will not be disadvantaged for raising a complaint

## Quality Indicators

- Written in plain, parent-friendly language
- Visual flowchart or step-by-step summary showing the stages clearly
- Specific timescales at each stage (not vague "reasonable time" phrasing)
- Downloadable complaint form or clear template
- Contact details (name, email, address) for submitting a complaint
- Links to ESFA or Ombudsman external escalation routes
- Published as a standalone page or clearly labelled document, not buried in a policy pack
- Explains what types of issues are NOT covered by the complaints procedure (e.g. admissions appeals, exclusions, staff grievances)

## Red Flags

- No complaints procedure published on the website
- Procedure has no timescales at any stage
- No mention of governing body panel hearing stage
- No external escalation route mentioned (ESFA, Ombudsman, Ofsted)
- References to EFA instead of ESFA (outdated)
- Procedure makes raising a complaint appear discouraging or hostile
- No named contact or role responsible for complaints
- Academy procedure references LA complaints process instead of ESFA
- Panel composition not specified or allows members who investigated earlier stages

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Complaints procedure published and accessible on website
- All stages clearly described (informal, formal, panel, external)
- Timescales stated at each stage
- Named contact or role for complaints identified
- External escalation routes clearly stated and correct for school type
- Review date within last 14 months

### Partial (40-79)

- Procedure exists but missing one or more stages
- Timescales vague or absent at some stages
- No panel hearing details
- External escalation route missing or incorrect for school type
- Outdated but broadly accurate content

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No complaints procedure found on website
- Only a brief statement with no staged process
- Procedure is significantly outdated (3+ years) or references defunct bodies
- Content does not describe how to actually make a complaint

## Notes

- Academies and maintained schools have different external escalation routes.
  Academies escalate to the ESFA; maintained schools to the LA and then the
  Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman. Multi-academy trusts may have
  a trust-level complaints procedure with the individual school procedure nested within.
- Ofsted does not investigate individual complaints but can be contacted if the
  school is not meeting its statutory duties.
- Some schools combine this with a "Concerns and Complaints" page which handles
  informal concerns and formal complaints together. This is acceptable provided
  the formal staged process is still clearly described.
- The DfE best practice guidance is advisory, not statutory, but Ofsted inspectors
  will check that a complaints procedure is published and accessible.
