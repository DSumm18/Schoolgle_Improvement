# SENCO Details

## Legislation

- SEND Code of Practice 2015 (Chapter 6, Sections 6.79-6.91)
- Special Educational Needs and Disability Regulations 2014, Regulation 51
- Children and Families Act 2014, Section 67

## Current References

Must align with the SEND Code of Practice 2015. While the Code dates
from 2015, it remains the current statutory guidance. The SENCO role
requirements were strengthened by the 2014 Regulations, which require
schools to publish the SENCO's name and contact details on their website.

Schools should be aware of the SEND and AP Improvement Plan (March 2023)
which signals forthcoming changes to the SENCO role, including a potential
new National Professional Qualification for SENCOs.

## Review Frequency

Annually, or whenever the SENCO changes. Must be kept up to date at all
times — this is contact information that parents rely on. Should be
verified at the start of each academic year.

## Required Content

1. Full name of the SENCO (first name and surname, not just initials)
2. Direct contact method — a dedicated email address or direct phone
   number for the SENCO (not just "contact the school office" or a
   generic info@ address)
3. The SENCO's professional qualifications, specifically whether they
   hold the National Award for SEN Coordination (mandatory for SENCOs
   appointed after 1 September 2009, with a 3-year grace period)
4. Confirmation that the SENCO is a qualified teacher (statutory
   requirement under SEND Code of Practice 6.84)

## Quality Indicators

- Photograph of the SENCO
- Brief professional biography or description of experience
- Stated availability (e.g. days in school if part-time, best times to contact)
- Named SEND Governor alongside the SENCO
- Description of the SENCO's role and what they can help with
- Information about the wider SEND team (teaching assistants, specialists)
- Link to the SEN Information Report from the SENCO details page
- Invitation to parents to make contact with a welcoming tone
- Details of any SEND drop-in sessions or parent surgery times

## Red Flags

- No SENCO named anywhere on the website
- Only a job title given with no name (e.g. "Our SENCO can be contacted...")
- Generic office contact only — no direct route to the SENCO
- SENCO listed but with no contact details at all
- Name appears outdated (e.g. staff member who has left the school)
- No mention of SENCO qualifications or National Award status
- SENCO details buried in a PDF document rather than visible on the website
- SENCO is listed as unqualified or non-teaching (breach of statutory requirement)

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- SENCO full name clearly published
- Direct email or phone number provided (not just a generic contact)
- National Award for SEN Coordination mentioned (held or working towards)
- Confirmed as a qualified teacher
- Information is current and easily findable on the website

### Partial (40-79)

- SENCO named but only a generic school contact provided
- Name published but no qualifications mentioned
- Details exist but are buried in a downloadable document
- Information appears potentially outdated
- Contact route requires going through the school office

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No SENCO named on the website
- No contact details for SEND at all
- Only a job title with no individual identified
- Information is clearly outdated (names a person no longer at the school)
- SENCO details are behind a login or password-protected area

## Notes

- SENCO details are often published within the SEN Information Report
  rather than as a standalone page. This is acceptable provided the
  information is clear and easy to find. However, best practice is to
  also include SENCO details on a staff or contact page.
- Since September 2009, all newly appointed SENCOs must gain the National
  Award for SEN Coordination within 3 years of appointment. Schools
  should state whether the SENCO holds this or is working towards it.
- The SEND Code of Practice (6.84) requires the SENCO to be a qualified
  teacher. In maintained schools this is a legal requirement. In
  academies, best practice is to follow the same standard.
- Part-time SENCOs should indicate their working days so parents know
  when they can expect a response.
- In MATs, each school must have its own named SENCO — a trust-level
  SEND lead does not replace the school SENCO requirement.
