# RSE Policy (Relationships and Sex Education)

## Legislation

- Children and Social Work Act 2017, Section 34
- Relationships Education, Relationships and Sex Education and Health Education (England) Regulations 2019
- DfE "Relationships Education, Relationships and Sex Education (RSE) and Health Education" statutory guidance (2019, updated 2024)
- DfE "Gender Questioning Children" guidance (December 2024)
- Equality Act 2010

## Current References

Must reference the DfE statutory guidance on Relationships Education, RSE and
Health Education (originally 2019). Should demonstrate awareness of the 2024
updates, particularly the DfE guidance on gender questioning children
(December 2024) which clarifies schools should not teach gender identity as a
concept. Any reference to PSHE Association resources should be current.
Primary schools should reference "Relationships Education" (not RSE, as sex
education is not compulsory in primary).

## Review Frequency

Annually, with parental consultation. The statutory guidance requires schools
to consult parents when developing the RSE policy and keep it up to date.
Must show a review date or academic year. Governing body approval required.

## Required Content

1. Statement that Relationships Education is compulsory in all primary schools and RSE is compulsory in all secondary schools
2. What is taught — age-appropriate curriculum content for each key stage or year group
3. Primary: Relationships Education content — families, friendships, respectful relationships, online safety, being safe
4. Secondary: RSE content — all of the above plus intimate relationships, consent, contraception, sexual health, online and media (age-appropriate)
5. Health Education content — mental wellbeing, internet safety, physical health, healthy eating, drugs/alcohol/tobacco, basic first aid
6. Parent right to withdraw — parents can request withdrawal from sex education (not Relationships Education, not Health Education) up to three terms before the child turns 16, at which point the child can opt in
7. Process for requesting withdrawal — who to contact, what happens (meeting with headteacher to discuss, child's wishes considered)
8. How the school ensures the policy is inclusive and respects the protected characteristics under the Equality Act 2010
9. How the school handles sensitive topics — ground rules, distancing techniques, managing disclosures
10. Reference to the school's approach to gender questioning children in line with DfE guidance (December 2024) — schools should not teach gender identity ideology as fact
11. How parents can view curriculum materials
12. Staff training and who delivers RSE
13. How the RSE curriculum is monitored and assessed

## Quality Indicators

- Written in plain language accessible to parents from all backgrounds
- Year-by-year or key-stage breakdown of what is taught
- Named RSE lead or coordinator
- Evidence of parental consultation (date, method, how feedback was incorporated)
- Links to curriculum resources or schemes of work used
- Sensitive and balanced approach to diverse family structures
- Cross-references to safeguarding policy, online safety policy, and behaviour policy
- Explains how pupils with SEND are supported in RSE lessons
- Published as an accessible webpage with downloadable full policy
- FAQ section addressing common parent concerns

## Red Flags

- No RSE policy published on the website
- Policy does not mention parent right to withdraw from sex education
- Primary school policy refers to "sex education" as compulsory (it is not compulsory in primary)
- No mention of what is actually taught (just generic statements)
- Policy promotes gender identity ideology as established fact (contrary to DfE 2024 guidance)
- No evidence of parental consultation
- Policy is a generic template with no school-specific content
- No mention of Equality Act protected characteristics
- Content not age-appropriate or no differentiation by key stage
- Policy predates 2019 statutory guidance
- No mention of online safety in the context of relationships

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Policy published and accessible on website
- Curriculum content described by key stage or year group
- Parent right to withdraw from sex education clearly stated with process
- Evidence of parental consultation
- References current DfE statutory guidance (2019/2024)
- Inclusive approach with Equality Act awareness
- Review date within last 14 months

### Partial (40-79)

- Policy exists but curriculum content is vague
- Right to withdraw mentioned but process unclear
- No evidence of parental consultation
- Missing DfE 2024 gender guidance reference
- Generic template not customised to school
- Missing 2-3 required elements

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No RSE policy found on website
- Policy does not describe what is taught
- No mention of parent right to withdraw
- Policy is significantly outdated (predates 2019 guidance)
- Brief statement with no substantive content

## Notes

- This is one of the most scrutinised policies on school websites. Parents,
  campaign groups, and inspectors all check this policy carefully.
- The critical distinction is: Relationships Education (compulsory, all schools),
  Sex Education (compulsory secondary, optional primary), Health Education
  (compulsory, all schools). Parents can only withdraw from sex education.
- The DfE "Gender Questioning Children" guidance (December 2024) is significant.
  Schools should not teach contested views on gender identity as established fact
  and should take a cautious approach. Policies should reflect this.
- Faith schools may teach RSE within the tenets of their faith but must still
  comply with the Equality Act 2010. The RSE policy should reflect this balance.
- Many schools use published schemes (e.g. PSHE Association, Jigsaw, 1decision)
  which can be referenced, but the policy should still describe what is taught
  rather than just naming the scheme.
- Parental consultation is a statutory requirement, not just good practice. Schools
  should be able to evidence when and how they consulted parents.
