# Register of Interests and Family Relationships

## Legislation

- Academy Trust Handbook 2025 (Sections on related party transactions
  and conflicts of interest)
- Companies Act 2006, Section 177 (duty to declare interests)
- Charity Commission guidance on conflicts of interest (CC29)
- Academies Act 2010

## Current References

Must comply with the Academy Trust Handbook 2025. The Handbook requires
academy trusts to maintain a register of interests for trustees and
senior employees, including business and personal interests and family
relationships with other trustees or employees. The register must be
published on the trust's website.

The ESFA strengthened these requirements following high-profile cases
of related party transactions. Trusts should also be aware of the ESFA's
related party transactions reporting requirements and prior approval
thresholds.

## Review Frequency

At least annually. The register must be updated whenever a new interest
or relationship is declared or an existing one changes. Best practice
is to review the register at the start of each academic year and at
every board meeting (as a standing agenda item). Trustees and senior
staff should be required to confirm their declarations are up to date
at least annually.

## Required Content

1. Names of all trustees (directors) with their declared business
   and personal interests — including directorships, shareholdings,
   employment, and any relationships with organisations that do
   business with the trust
2. Names of senior employees (CEO, CFO, headteachers, senior leaders
   at trust level) with their declared interests
3. Family relationships between trustees, local governors, and
   employees of the trust — specifically where a trustee/governor
   has a family member employed by the trust, or where two employees
   in a line-management or procurement relationship are related
4. A "nil return" for any individual with no interests to declare
   (absence from the register should not mean "no interests" — it
   could mean the return was not completed)
5. Date the register was last updated

## Quality Indicators

- Full register published as a table or structured document (not just
  a statement saying "a register is maintained")
- Covers all required categories: business interests, financial
  interests, governance roles at other organisations, family
  relationships
- Nil returns explicitly recorded (shows completeness)
- Historical declarations retained or archived
- Clear policy or procedure document explaining how interests are
  declared and managed
- Register covers local governors as well as trustees
- Related party transactions policy published alongside the register
- Process for managing conflicts of interest described
- Evidence that the register is used actively (e.g. referenced in
  board minutes)
- Links to the trust's procurement and financial procedures

## Red Flags

- No register of interests published on the website
- Register exists but does not include family relationships
- Only trustees listed — senior employees omitted
- No nil returns — gaps could indicate incomplete declarations
- Register is clearly outdated (lists former trustees, no recent date)
- Trust states "no related party interests" for all individuals
  without evidence of actual declarations
- Register published as an inaccessible scanned PDF
- Family relationships between trustees/governors and employees not
  disclosed despite being known
- Same surname appearing in trustee and employee lists with no
  relationship declared
- No date on the register

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Register published on the website covering trustees and senior staff
- Business interests, personal interests, and family relationships
  all covered
- Nil returns recorded for individuals with no interests
- Current date showing recent review
- Clear and readable format (table or structured list)
- Covers family relationships between governance and staff

### Partial (40-79)

- Register exists but only covers business interests (family
  relationships omitted)
- Only trustees included — senior employees missing
- Register published but not recently updated
- Format is unclear or difficult to interpret
- No nil returns — unclear whether gaps mean "no interest" or
  "not completed"
- Partial coverage of required categories

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No register of interests published on the website
- Only a generic statement that "a register is maintained" with no
  actual data published
- Register is more than 2 years old
- Family relationships not addressed at all
- Register is behind a login or not publicly accessible
- Clearly incomplete (only 1-2 individuals listed for a large trust)

## Notes

- This requirement applies to ACADEMIES AND FREE SCHOOLS ONLY.
  Maintained schools are encouraged to publish a register of interests
  but it is not a statutory requirement in the same way.
- The Academy Trust Handbook specifically requires disclosure of family
  relationships. This goes beyond standard company law requirements
  and reflects the public accountability of academy trusts.
- "Family relationships" includes spouse/partner, parent, child,
  sibling, and any other close family member. The Handbook does not
  define "close" precisely, so trusts should take a broad approach.
- Related party transactions above the ESFA threshold require prior
  approval from the ESFA. The register of interests supports the
  trust's ability to identify and manage these transactions.
- For MATs, the register should cover all trustees and senior employees
  across the trust, not just those at individual schools. However,
  local governing body members should also be included.
- Common issue: trusts maintain the register but do not publish it.
  The Academy Trust Handbook requires publication on the website, not
  just internal maintenance.
- The register should be a living document, not a once-a-year exercise.
  Trusts should have a procedure for in-year declarations when new
  interests arise.
- Auditors and the ESFA may request to see the register during
  financial audits. Having it published on the website provides
  transparency and reduces audit friction.
