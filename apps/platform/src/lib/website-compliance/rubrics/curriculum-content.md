# Curriculum Content

## Legislation

- School Information (England) Regulations 2008, Schedule 4, Part 1
- Academies Act 2010 (academies must publish curriculum information)
- Academy Trust Handbook 2025, Section 2.5 (for academies)
- Equality Act 2010 (curriculum accessibility)

## Current References

Must reference the current academic year (e.g. 2025-26). Curriculum
information that only refers to a previous academic year without the
current year being covered indicates the page has not been updated.

Schools should show their curriculum is aligned to the National Curriculum
(maintained schools) or demonstrate breadth and balance (academies).

## Review Frequency

Annually at the start of each academic year. Should be updated to reflect
the current year's curriculum offer. No statutory review body requirement,
but headteacher should ensure accuracy.

## Required Content

1. Content of the curriculum in each academic year — what is taught in each
   year group, not just key stage summaries
2. Subjects offered in each year group (including statutory subjects:
   English, maths, science, computing, PE, and foundation subjects)
3. Curriculum intent — the school's aims and rationale for its curriculum
   design, what it wants pupils to know and be able to do
4. Curriculum implementation — how the curriculum is delivered, teaching
   approaches, sequencing and progression
5. Curriculum impact — how the school measures whether the curriculum is
   achieving its aims
6. How the curriculum is adapted for pupils with SEND, including how
   reasonable adjustments are made
7. Enrichment and extra-curricular activities offered (clubs, trips,
   visiting speakers, cultural experiences)
8. For academies: confirmation that the curriculum is broad and balanced
   even if not following the National Curriculum in full

## Quality Indicators

- Detailed curriculum maps or long-term plans for each subject and year group
- Knowledge organisers showing key vocabulary, concepts, and facts per unit
- Parent-friendly guides explaining what children learn each term
- Clear progression model showing how knowledge and skills build year on year
- Named subject leaders for each curriculum area
- Links between curriculum and the school's context (local area, cohort needs)
- Explanation of how curriculum addresses cultural capital and British Values
- Homework expectations published alongside curriculum content
- Cross-curricular links and SMSC/character education integration shown
- Reading spine or recommended reading lists linked to curriculum topics

## Red Flags

- No curriculum information found anywhere on the website
- Only a single generic statement about curriculum (e.g. "We follow the
  National Curriculum") with no detail about what is taught
- Curriculum pages exist but are empty or contain placeholder text
- No year group or subject breakdown — just key stage summaries
- No mention of SEND adaptation or accessibility
- Curriculum information is clearly from a previous academic year and has
  not been updated
- Only PDF downloads with no indication of content or date
- Missing statutory subjects (e.g. no mention of computing, RE, or RSHE)
- Curriculum content appears to be copied from another school without
  adaptation to the school's own context

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Curriculum content published for each year group or key stage
- Subjects offered are clearly listed
- Curriculum intent, implementation, and impact are described
- SEND adaptation is addressed
- Enrichment and extra-curricular provision is published
- Information relates to the current academic year
- Content is organised and navigable (not buried in hard-to-find pages)

### Partial (40-79)

- Curriculum information exists but one or more of:
  - Only covers some year groups or some subjects
  - No SEND adaptation mentioned
  - No enrichment or extra-curricular information
  - Generic intent statement without implementation or impact detail
  - Information is present but clearly outdated (previous year only)
  - Content is in PDF-only format with no overview on the page itself

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No curriculum content published on the website
- Only a vague statement (e.g. "We teach a broad curriculum") with no
  subject or year group detail
- Pages exist with headings but no actual content
- Curriculum section links are broken or lead to empty pages

## Notes

- Many schools organise curriculum information by subject (one page per
  subject) or by year group (one page per year). Both approaches are
  acceptable as long as coverage is comprehensive.
- Academies are not legally required to follow the National Curriculum but
  must provide a broad and balanced curriculum and must still publish what
  they teach.
- Ofsted inspectors routinely check the website for curriculum information
  as part of their pre-inspection research. A well-published curriculum
  signals strong leadership.
- Some schools use third-party curriculum platforms (e.g. Cornerstones,
  Kapow) and link to them. This is acceptable as long as the school-specific
  implementation is clear, not just the scheme's generic content.
- RSHE (Relationships, Sex and Health Education) is now statutory and
  should appear in the curriculum content. Schools must also publish their
  RSHE policy separately.
- The School Information Regulations require "content of the curriculum
  followed by the school" — this means what is actually taught, not just
  policy statements about curriculum.
