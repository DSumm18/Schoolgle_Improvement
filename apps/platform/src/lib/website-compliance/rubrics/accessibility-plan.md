# Accessibility Plan

## Legislation

- Equality Act 2010, Schedule 10 (Schools: accessibility)
- Disability Discrimination Act 1995 (predecessor legislation, still referenced)
- School Information (England) Regulations 2008, Schedule 4
- SEND Code of Practice 2015

## Current References

Must comply with the Equality Act 2010. The duty to prepare and publish
an accessibility plan has been in force since 2002 (originally under the
DDA 1995, now under the Equality Act 2010 Schedule 10). The plan should
reference the Equality Act 2010 as the governing legislation.

Schools should also be aware of the Public Sector Equality Duty (Section
149 of the Equality Act 2010) which underpins the requirement to advance
equality of opportunity for disabled pupils.

## Review Frequency

The plan must cover a 3-year period and be reviewed and revised as
necessary. The governing body must approve the plan. Schools should
indicate the plan period (e.g. 2024-2027) and the date of the last
review. Annual progress reviews against the plan are best practice.

## Required Content

The Equality Act 2010 Schedule 10 requires the plan to address three
key areas:

1. Increasing the extent to which disabled pupils can participate in
   the school's curriculum — strategies for making lessons and learning
   activities accessible (differentiation, specialist equipment, staff
   training, alternative formats)
2. Improving the physical environment of the school to increase the
   extent to which disabled pupils can take advantage of education and
   associated services — building access, classroom layout, sensory
   environment, signage, toileting and changing facilities
3. Improving the delivery to disabled pupils of information that is
   readily accessible to other pupils — how the school provides
   information in alternative formats (large print, audio, Braille,
   easy-read, translated materials) for disabled pupils and their parents
4. A clear 3-year timeframe with start and end dates for the plan
5. Specific, measurable actions with responsible persons and target dates
6. Evidence of review — what has been achieved since the last plan

## Quality Indicators

- Clear 3-year plan period stated (e.g. "2024-2027")
- Action plan format with columns for: action, responsibility, timeline,
  cost, success criteria, progress/outcome
- An audit of current accessibility included (starting point assessment)
- Consultation with disabled pupils, parents, and staff evidenced
- Links to related policies (Equality Policy, SEND Policy, SEN
  Information Report)
- Specific budget allocation or cost estimates for actions
- Annual progress update published alongside the plan
- Reference to reasonable adjustments duty
- Coverage of both physical disabilities and non-physical disabilities
  (sensory, cognitive, mental health)
- Named accessibility lead or responsible governor

## Red Flags

- No accessibility plan published on the website
- Plan is expired (end date has passed with no updated version)
- Plan contains no specific actions — just generic statements of intent
- Only addresses physical access, ignoring curriculum and information
- No dates, no responsible persons, no measurable targets
- Plan is clearly a generic template with no school-specific content
- School states "we don't currently have any disabled pupils" as
  justification for a minimal plan (the duty is anticipatory)
- Plan is more than 3 years old with no evidence of review
- Confuses the accessibility plan with the website accessibility statement
  (they are different documents)

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- All three statutory areas addressed (curriculum, physical, information)
- Current 3-year plan period clearly stated
- Specific actions with responsible persons and timelines
- Evidence of review or progress reporting
- Approved by governing body
- School-specific content, not a generic template

### Partial (40-79)

- Plan exists but only covers one or two of the three statutory areas
- Actions are vague or lack timelines and responsible persons
- Plan period is unclear or has expired within the last year
- Some school-specific content but largely template-based
- No evidence of progress review

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No accessibility plan found on the website
- Plan is more than 3 years old with no update
- Document exists but contains no specific actions
- Only a policy statement with no plan of action
- Generic template with no school-specific content whatsoever

## Notes

- The accessibility plan is a separate statutory requirement from the
  website accessibility statement (Public Sector Bodies Accessibility
  Regulations 2018). Schools need both documents.
- The duty is anticipatory — schools must plan for potential future
  disabled pupils, not just respond to current needs.
- Maintained schools: the governing body must prepare the plan.
  Academies: the academy trust must prepare the plan, but it should
  be school-specific.
- Many schools combine their accessibility plan with their Equality
  Policy or publish it as an appendix. This is acceptable provided
  the plan element is clearly identifiable with specific actions.
- The plan should cover all protected characteristics related to
  disability, including physical, sensory, learning, and mental health
  conditions.
- Schools often publish this as a PDF. While acceptable, a web page
  version is more accessible (which is somewhat ironic if the
  accessibility plan itself is not accessible).
