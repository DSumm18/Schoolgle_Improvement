# Gender Pay Gap Report

## Legislation

- Equality Act 2010 (Gender Pay Gap Information) Regulations 2017
- Equality Act 2010 (Specific Duties and Public Authorities)
  Regulations 2017

## Current References

Must publish gender pay gap data using the snapshot date of 31 March
each year (for public sector employers). The report must be published
by 30 March of the following year. For the 2025-26 reporting cycle,
the snapshot date is 31 March 2025, with publication required by
30 March 2026.

## Review Frequency

Annually. Data is captured on the snapshot date (31 March for public
sector) and must be published within 12 months. Reports should remain
on the website alongside previous years' data.

## Required Content

1. Mean gender pay gap in hourly pay (percentage)
2. Median gender pay gap in hourly pay (percentage)
3. Mean bonus gender pay gap (percentage)
4. Median bonus gender pay gap (percentage)
5. Proportion of males and females receiving a bonus payment
6. Proportion of males and females in each pay quartile
   (lower, lower middle, upper middle, upper)
7. The snapshot date the data relates to
8. A written statement confirming the data is accurate, signed by
   an appropriate senior person

## Quality Indicators

- Accompanying narrative explaining the reasons for any pay gap
- Action plan describing steps being taken to reduce the gap
- Year-on-year trend data showing progress
- Breakdown by school/academy within a MAT
- Context about the workforce (e.g. high proportion of part-time
  female staff in lower-paid roles)
- Published on the government's gender pay gap reporting service
  as well as the school/trust website
- Easy-to-understand visualisations (charts, infographics)

## Red Flags

- No gender pay gap report found on the website (for employers
  with 250+ staff)
- Report is more than one year out of date
- Missing one or more of the six mandatory data points
- No confirmation statement or signature
- Data appears implausible (e.g. 0% gap when workforce is
  predominantly female in lower-paid roles)
- Report published on the government portal but not on the
  employer's own website
- Bonus gap data missing even though bonuses are paid

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- All six mandatory data points published
- Data relates to the most recent snapshot date
- Signed confirmation statement included
- Published on the school/trust website
- Snapshot date clearly stated

### Partial (40-79)

- Report exists but:
  - Is one year out of date
  - Missing some mandatory data points (e.g. bonus gap or quartiles)
  - No signed confirmation statement
  - Published on the government portal but not on own website
  - Narrative provided but specific data points missing

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No gender pay gap report found (for an employer with 250+ staff)
- Report is 2+ years out of date
- Only a general equality statement with no specific pay gap data
- Employer states they are exempt when they are not

## Notes

- This requirement only applies to employers with 250 or more
  employees on the snapshot date. Most individual schools will be
  below this threshold. Multi-academy trusts with 250+ staff
  across the trust are required to report.
- The snapshot date for public sector employers (including schools
  and academy trusts) is 31 March. Private sector employers use
  5 April.
- Schools/trusts must publish on their own website AND on the
  government's gender pay gap reporting service at
  https://gender-pay-gap.service.gov.uk/
- Teaching and non-teaching staff should be included in the same
  report.
- The report does not require identifying individual staff members.
  It is aggregate data only.
- Many schools below the 250-staff threshold choose to publish
  voluntarily as good practice. This is commendable but not
  required.
- Bonuses are uncommon in schools, so the bonus gap figures are
  often zero or N/A. This should still be explicitly stated.
