# Careers Programme

## Legislation

- Careers guidance and access for education and training providers
  (DfE, January 2023)
- Education Act 1997, Section 42A-42B (as amended by Technical and
  Further Education Act 2017)
- School Information (England) Regulations 2008, Schedule 4
- Baker Clause — Education Act 1997, Section 42B (provider access
  legislation, strengthened January 2023)
- Academy Trust Handbook 2025, Section 2.5 (for academies)

## Current References

Must reflect the strengthened provider access legislation (Baker Clause)
effective from January 2023, which requires schools to provide at least
6 encounters with providers of approved technical education qualifications
or apprenticeships across Years 7-13.

The Gatsby Benchmarks remain the framework for good careers guidance.
Schools should reference these. Ofsted uses the Gatsby Benchmarks when
evaluating careers provision.

## Review Frequency

Annually. The careers programme should be reviewed and updated at the
start of each academic year. The provider access policy statement must
be current and reflect the school's actual arrangements.

## Required Content

1. Name and contact details of the school's Careers Leader — this is a
   statutory requirement since September 2018
2. Provider access policy statement — how education and training providers
   can request access to pupils, as required by the Baker Clause. Must include:
   a. Specific times during the year when providers can speak to pupils
   b. How the school manages provider access requests
   c. The premises and facilities available for providers
   d. A named contact for access requests
3. How the school meets each of the 8 Gatsby Benchmarks:
   1. A stable careers programme
   2. Learning from career and labour market information
   3. Addressing the needs of each pupil
   4. Linking curriculum learning to careers
   5. Encounters with employers and employees
   6. Experiences of workplaces
   7. Encounters with further and higher education
   8. Personal guidance
4. Careers curriculum by year group — what careers education happens in
   each year from Year 7 to Year 13 (or Year 11 for schools without sixth form)
5. Work experience provision — when and how work experience is arranged
   (typically Year 10 and/or Year 12)
6. How the programme is funded and resourced

## Quality Indicators

- Compass or Compass+ results showing the school's self-assessment against
  Gatsby Benchmarks (Compass is the free DfE tool for this)
- Named employers and organisations the school partners with
- Details of careers events (careers fairs, employer talks, visits)
- Information about the careers library or online resources available
  to students
- Specific provision for SEND pupils and how careers guidance is adapted
- How parents are involved in careers education
- Destination data showing where former pupils have progressed to
- Links to external careers resources (National Careers Service, UCAS,
  Unifrog, etc.)
- Details of any careers-related qualifications offered (e.g. CEIAG
  certificate, enterprise qualifications)
- Mention of how disadvantaged pupils are prioritised for careers support
- Details of 1:1 personal guidance provision (who delivers it, qualifications)
- Labour market information relevant to the school's local area

## Red Flags

- No careers programme information on the website
- No named Careers Leader
- No provider access policy statement (Baker Clause non-compliance)
- Careers information consists only of a link to an external website
  with no school-specific content
- No year-group breakdown of careers provision
- Work experience not mentioned for a school with Year 10/12 pupils
- Gatsby Benchmarks not referenced or addressed
- Provider access policy exists but gives no specific access times or
  makes access unreasonably difficult
- Careers page is clearly out of date (e.g. references events from
  2+ years ago)
- Only university-focused information with no mention of apprenticeships,
  technical education, or vocational pathways
- No mention of personal guidance (1:1 careers interviews)

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Named Careers Leader with contact details published
- Provider access policy statement published with required detail
  (times, process, premises, contact)
- Gatsby Benchmarks referenced and the school's approach to each described
- Careers curriculum by year group published
- Work experience provision described
- Programme relates to the current academic year
- Information is comprehensive and easy to navigate

### Partial (40-79)

- Careers information exists but one or more of:
  - No named Careers Leader
  - No provider access policy statement
  - Gatsby Benchmarks not addressed
  - No year-group breakdown of provision
  - Work experience not mentioned
  - Information is out of date
  - Content is sparse (e.g. just a page with a few bullet points)
  - Provider access policy exists but lacks required specifics

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No careers programme information found on the website
- Only a brief mention of careers with no substantive content
- Only a link to an external careers website with no school-specific
  information
- Page exists with a heading but no content
- Careers Leader not named and no programme described

## Notes

- This requirement applies to SECONDARY schools only (with Year 7+).
  Primary schools are not required to publish careers programme information,
  though some choose to publish information about aspirations and enterprise
  activities.
- The Baker Clause was significantly strengthened in January 2023. Schools
  must now provide at least 6 encounters with providers across Years 7-13,
  with at least 2 in each of Years 8-9, Years 10-11, and Years 12-13.
- Ofsted assesses careers provision under the Personal Development judgement.
  Inspectors will check the website for the careers programme and provider
  access policy.
- The Careers Leader should be a member of school staff (not an external
  provider), though external careers advisers can supplement the provision.
  The Careers Leader should ideally be trained (e.g. through the Careers
  Leader training funded by the Careers & Enterprise Company).
- "Encounters with providers" means face-to-face or virtual meetings where
  pupils learn about technical and vocational routes. A school that only
  promotes university is not meeting the Baker Clause requirements.
- Many schools use Unifrog, Start, or similar platforms to deliver and
  track careers provision. Mentioning the platform used is helpful but
  should not replace describing the actual programme.
- Schools in Careers Hubs (coordinated by the Careers & Enterprise Company)
  often perform better against Gatsby Benchmarks. Mention of Careers Hub
  membership is a positive indicator.
- Special schools and AP (alternative provision) must also provide careers
  guidance appropriate to their pupils' needs.
