# Equality Objectives

## Legislation

- Equality Act 2010, Section 149 (Public Sector Equality Duty)
- Equality Act 2010 (Specific Duties) Regulations 2011, Regulation 3

## Current References

Must publish one or more specific and measurable equality objectives
at least every four years. Objectives should be reviewed and updated
to reflect current priorities. Objectives set before 2022 are likely
out of date unless explicitly renewed.

## Review Frequency

At least every four years. Schools must prepare and publish new
objectives by the end of the four-year cycle. Many schools review
annually as part of their equality policy review, which is good
practice. The governing body should formally approve the objectives.

## Required Content

1. One or more specific, measurable equality objectives
2. Which protected characteristics each objective relates to
   (age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage/civil partnership,
   pregnancy/maternity, race, religion/belief, sex, sexual orientation)
3. How progress towards each objective will be measured
4. The date the objectives were set or last reviewed
5. The period the objectives cover (e.g. 2024-2028)

## Quality Indicators

- Objectives are genuinely specific and measurable (not vague
  aspirations like "promote equality")
- Progress against previous objectives is reported
- Objectives are informed by the school's equality information
  and data analysis
- Clear link between identified inequalities and chosen objectives
- Objectives cover multiple protected characteristics, not just
  one or two
- Consultation with staff, pupils, parents, and community evidenced
- Objectives align with the school improvement plan
- Named lead responsible for each objective
- Success criteria defined for each objective

## Red Flags

- No equality objectives published on the website
- Objectives are more than four years old with no evidence of renewal
- Objectives are vague and unmeasurable (e.g. "we will promote
  equality and diversity")
- Only one protected characteristic addressed
- No mention of how progress will be measured
- Objectives are identical to a template found on other school
  websites with no school-specific context
- Objectives only address race and sex, ignoring other protected
  characteristics (disability, religion, sexual orientation, etc.)

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- One or more specific, measurable equality objectives published
- Objectives are within the four-year cycle (not expired)
- Protected characteristics addressed are identified
- How progress will be measured is described
- Date of setting/review is stated

### Partial (40-79)

- Objectives exist but:
  - Are vague and not measurable
  - Are close to or past the four-year expiry
  - Do not specify which protected characteristics they address
  - No progress measurement described
  - Buried within a broader equality policy rather than clearly
    identified as specific objectives

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No equality objectives found on the website
- Objectives are more than four years old with no renewal
- Only a general equality statement with no specific objectives
- Website states "objectives available on request" without publishing

## Notes

- The Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED) applies to all state-funded
  schools — both maintained schools and academies.
- The duty requires schools to have "due regard" to the need to:
  eliminate discrimination, advance equality of opportunity, and
  foster good relations between people with different protected
  characteristics.
- Equality objectives are separate from (but should complement) the
  school's equality information (published annually — see
  equality-information rubric).
- Schools are NOT required to have objectives covering every
  protected characteristic. However, they should consider all nine
  characteristics and explain their choice of priorities.
- The Equality and Human Rights Commission (EHRC) can take
  enforcement action against schools that fail to publish objectives.
- Many schools combine their equality objectives with their equality
  policy. This is acceptable as long as the objectives are clearly
  identifiable as specific, measurable commitments.
- Common strong objectives include: reducing attainment gaps for
  specific groups, increasing representation in leadership,
  improving accessibility of the building, increasing participation
  in extra-curricular activities for disadvantaged groups.
