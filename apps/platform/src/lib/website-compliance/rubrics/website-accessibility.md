# Website Accessibility Statement

## Legislation

- Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) Accessibility
  Regulations 2018
- Equality Act 2010
- EN 301 549 (European accessibility standard)
- Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA

## Current References

Must comply with the Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations 2018.
The statement must reference WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the target standard.
Schools should use the GDS (Government Digital Service) model accessibility
statement as a basis. Since September 2020, all public sector websites
(including school websites) must meet WCAG 2.1 AA and publish a compliant
accessibility statement.

The statement must be reviewed and updated at least annually, and
whenever significant changes are made to the website.

## Review Frequency

Annually at minimum. Must also be updated when significant website
changes are made, when new accessibility issues are discovered, or
when previously reported issues are resolved. The statement must
include the date it was last reviewed.

## Required Content

The Accessibility Regulations require the statement to include:

1. A declaration of which parts of the website are not accessible
   and the specific WCAG 2.1 AA criteria that are not met
2. An explanation for each non-compliance (e.g. disproportionate
   burden, content not within scope of the regulations)
3. A description of any accessible alternatives provided
4. A contact mechanism for users to report accessibility problems
   (name, email, phone — not just a generic contact form)
5. A link to the Equality Advisory Support Service (EASS) or the
   Equality and Human Rights Commission (EHRC) enforcement procedure
   for users who are not satisfied with the school's response
6. The date the statement was prepared and last reviewed
7. The method used to assess accessibility (e.g. internal audit,
   external audit, automated tools used)
8. A statement of compliance level: fully compliant, partially
   compliant, or not compliant with WCAG 2.1 AA

## Quality Indicators

- Follows the GDS model accessibility statement template structure
- Specific WCAG criteria cited for each known issue (e.g. "1.1.1
  Non-text Content" rather than just "some images lack alt text")
- Timeline for fixing known issues included
- Evidence of professional accessibility audit (not just automated scan)
- Statement is itself accessible (published as HTML, not only as a PDF)
- Easy to find — linked from every page footer
- Contact details include multiple channels (email, phone, postal)
- Regular review schedule documented
- Lists specific assistive technologies tested (screen readers, voice
  recognition, magnification)
- Mentions disproportionate burden assessment where applicable

## Red Flags

- No accessibility statement published on the website
- Statement is a generic template with no school-specific content
- No known issues listed (extremely unlikely for any website —
  suggests the school has not actually audited their site)
- No contact details for reporting accessibility issues
- No link to EHRC/EASS enforcement procedure
- Statement has never been reviewed (no review date)
- Statement claims full compliance with no evidence of assessment
- Published only as a PDF (inaccessible format for an accessibility
  document)
- No date on the statement
- Statement is more than 2 years old with no update

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Statement follows standard format (GDS template or equivalent)
- WCAG 2.1 AA compliance level clearly declared
- Known issues listed with specific WCAG criteria
- Contact details for accessibility issues provided
- Link to EHRC/EASS enforcement procedure included
- Date of preparation and last review stated
- Assessment method described
- Updated within the last 12 months

### Partial (40-79)

- Statement exists but missing key elements (no enforcement link,
  no specific issues listed, no compliance level declared)
- Generic template partially customised
- Contact details vague or generic
- No review date or outdated (12-24 months)
- Does not cite specific WCAG criteria for known issues
- Assessment method not described

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No accessibility statement on the website
- Statement is more than 2 years old
- Statement is a single sentence or paragraph with no substantive content
- No contact mechanism for accessibility issues
- No reference to WCAG standards at all
- Published only as an inaccessible PDF with no HTML version

## Notes

- The website accessibility statement is different from the school's
  accessibility plan (Equality Act 2010 Schedule 10). The accessibility
  statement relates to the website; the accessibility plan relates to
  the physical school and curriculum. Schools need both.
- The GDS model accessibility statement is available at
  gov.uk/government/publications/sample-accessibility-statement. Schools
  should adapt this template rather than creating their own format.
- Schools can claim "disproportionate burden" for some fixes, but must
  carry out an assessment and explain why in the statement. This cannot
  be used as a blanket exemption.
- Third-party content (e.g. embedded videos, third-party booking systems)
  that the school cannot control may be noted as out of scope, but the
  school should still try to ensure accessibility where possible.
- Common issues for school websites: PDF documents without tags, images
  without alt text, poor colour contrast, forms without labels, videos
  without captions.
- The statement should be linked from every page — typically in the
  footer. It should not be buried in a policies section.
