# Whistleblowing Policy

## Legislation

- Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 (PIDA)
- Employment Rights Act 1996, Part IVA (as amended by PIDA)
- Education (Independent School Standards) Regulations 2014 (academies)
- Academy Trust Handbook 2025 (ESFA requirement for academies)
- KCSIE 2025 (staff duty to report safeguarding concerns)

## Current References

Must reference the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998. Academies must
demonstrate compliance with the Academy Trust Handbook requirement to maintain
a whistleblowing procedure. Should reference KCSIE 2025 for safeguarding-related
disclosures. References to the ESFA and external prescribed persons list should
be current (gov.uk links, not legacy contacts).

## Review Frequency

Annually, approved by governing body or trust board. Must show a review date
or academic year. Academies are required to have this as a condition of their
funding agreement and the Academy Trust Handbook.

## Required Content

1. Definition of whistleblowing — what it is and how it differs from a grievance or complaint
2. Scope — who can raise a concern (employees, governors, volunteers, agency workers, contractors, suppliers)
3. Types of concerns covered — criminal offences, failure to comply with legal obligations, miscarriages of justice, health and safety dangers, environmental damage, deliberate concealment of any of the above
4. Specific school-context examples — financial irregularity, safeguarding failures, exam malpractice, fraud, bribery, misuse of public funds
5. How to raise a concern internally — named contact or role (headteacher, chair of governors, trust CEO), method of reporting (written, verbal, email)
6. Alternative reporting routes when the concern involves the headteacher — chair of governors or trust board
7. Alternative reporting routes when the concern involves the governing body — ESFA, local authority, or appropriate prescribed person
8. External escalation — list of prescribed persons and regulators (ESFA, Ofsted, Health and Safety Executive, police, NSPCC, National Audit Office)
9. Protection for whistleblowers — statement that the school will not tolerate retaliation, harassment, or victimisation of anyone raising a genuine concern
10. Anonymity — whether anonymous reports are accepted and how they are handled
11. How concerns are investigated — process overview, timescales for acknowledgement and investigation
12. Record-keeping — how disclosures are recorded and stored confidentially
13. Outcome communication — how the whistleblower will be informed of the outcome (within confidentiality constraints)

## Quality Indicators

- Written in clear, accessible language (not dense legal text)
- Named contacts with actual names, roles, and contact details (not just "the headteacher")
- Multiple reporting channels offered (not just one route)
- Reassuring tone that actively encourages reporting of genuine concerns
- Clear distinction between whistleblowing and grievance procedures
- Step-by-step reporting process
- Reference to independent advice (e.g. Protect helpline — formerly Public Concern at Work)
- Published prominently (not buried in a staff handbook)
- Includes a statement that malicious or vexatious allegations may be treated differently

## Red Flags

- No whistleblowing policy published on the website (statutory for academies)
- Only one reporting route with no alternative if concern involves that person
- No mention of protection for whistleblowers
- No external escalation routes listed
- Policy describes whistleblowing in discouraging terms
- No mention of safeguarding-related concerns
- References to "Public Concern at Work" without noting rename to "Protect"
- Academy policy does not reference ESFA as an escalation route
- Policy is clearly a generic template with no school-specific contacts
- No mention of anonymity or confidentiality

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Policy published and accessible on website
- Clear definition of whistleblowing with scope of concerns covered
- Multiple internal reporting routes including alternative when concern involves headteacher
- External escalation routes correctly listed (ESFA, Ofsted, HSE, police)
- Protection statement for whistleblowers
- Named contacts or roles identified
- Review date within last 14 months

### Partial (40-79)

- Policy exists but missing external escalation routes
- Only one internal reporting route
- No protection statement for whistleblowers
- Generic template not customised with school contacts
- Missing 2-3 required elements
- Outdated but broadly accurate

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No whistleblowing policy found on website
- Policy exists but is a brief paragraph with no substance
- No reporting routes described
- Policy is significantly outdated (3+ years)

## Notes

- Publication on the school website is a statutory requirement for academies
  under the Academy Trust Handbook. For maintained schools it is strongly
  recommended but not technically a publication requirement — however, Ofsted
  inspectors will look for evidence that staff know how to raise concerns.
- The most common gap is failing to provide an alternative reporting route
  when the concern involves the headteacher or principal. This is critical —
  staff must have a route that bypasses the person they are concerned about.
- KCSIE 2025 emphasises that all staff have a duty to report safeguarding
  concerns and should know how to escalate if they feel their concern is not
  being acted upon. The whistleblowing policy should cross-reference this duty.
- Multi-academy trusts should have a trust-level whistleblowing policy that
  covers reporting to the CEO and trust board, as well as school-level contacts.
- "Protect" (formerly Public Concern at Work) is the UK's whistleblowing
  charity and offers a free confidential helpline. Referencing this in the
  policy is good practice.
