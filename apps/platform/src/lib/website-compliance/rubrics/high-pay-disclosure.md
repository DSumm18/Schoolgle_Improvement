# High Pay Disclosure (Over £100k)

## Legislation

- Academy Trust Handbook 2025, Section 2.4 (Executive Pay)
- Academies Accounts Direction (annual, issued by ESFA)

## Current References

Must disclose the number of employees whose total benefits exceeded
£100,000 in the most recent financial year, shown in £10,000 bands.
This disclosure should align with the latest published annual accounts.

## Review Frequency

Annually, updated when new audited accounts are published (by 31
December each year). The disclosure covers the same financial period
as the trust's annual accounts (typically September to August).

## Required Content

1. Number of employees whose total benefits (salary plus employer
   pension contributions plus other benefits) exceeded £100,000
2. Breakdown shown in £10,000 bands (e.g. £100,001-£110,000: 2 staff,
   £110,001-£120,000: 1 staff)
3. The financial period the disclosure covers
4. If no employees exceed £100,000, this should be explicitly stated

## Quality Indicators

- Published on a dedicated pay/governance page as well as in accounts
- Accompanied by a pay policy or pay committee terms of reference
- Context provided about the trust's approach to executive pay
- Explanation of what "total benefits" includes (salary, pension,
  benefits in kind)
- Comparison with the previous year's disclosure
- Statement from the board/trust on how executive pay is benchmarked
- CEO/Accounting Officer pay specifically identified

## Red Flags

- No high pay disclosure anywhere on the website or in accounts
- Disclosure exists in accounts but is for a prior financial year
  with no current update
- Only salary disclosed, not total benefits (pension contributions
  omitted)
- Bands are wrong (e.g. £20k bands instead of £10k bands)
- Trust clearly has highly paid staff but reports zero
- Gender pay gap data conflated with high pay disclosure

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Number of staff paid over £100k disclosed in £10,000 bands
- Covers the most recent financial year
- Published in the annual accounts and/or on the trust website
- Total benefits (not just salary) included in the calculation
- Financial period clearly stated

### Partial (40-79)

- Disclosure exists but:
  - Is one year out of date
  - Only appears in the annual accounts, not findable on website
  - Does not use £10k bands
  - Only shows salary, not total benefits
  - States "information available in accounts" without direct access

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No high pay disclosure found on the website or in published accounts
- Disclosure is 2+ years out of date
- Only a vague statement about pay with no band data
- Website explicitly avoids disclosing executive pay

## Notes

- This requirement applies to ACADEMIES ONLY. Maintained school
  staff pay is disclosed through local authority accounts.
- "Total benefits" includes gross salary, employer pension
  contributions, and any benefits in kind. This is typically higher
  than the headline salary figure.
- The disclosure is normally found in the notes to the annual
  accounts (usually Note 11 or 12). Some trusts also publish it
  separately on their governance or pay pages.
- The Academy Trust Handbook requires boards to ensure executive
  pay is "reasonable" and benchmarked. The disclosure is part of
  the transparency mechanism.
- ESFA has increasingly scrutinised high pay in academy trusts.
  The disclosure requirement helps stakeholders assess whether
  executive pay is proportionate to the trust's size and performance.
- If the trust has no employees paid over £100k, this should be
  explicitly stated rather than simply omitted.
