# SEN Information Report

## Legislation

- Children and Families Act 2014, Section 69
- SEND Code of Practice 2015 (Chapter 6, Section 6.79)
- School Information (England) Regulations 2008, Schedule 4
- Equality Act 2010 (reasonable adjustments)
- Special Educational Needs and Disability Regulations 2014, Regulation 51

## Current References

Must align with the SEND Code of Practice 2015. While the Code dates
from 2015, it remains the current statutory guidance. Schools should
also reference any recent SEND Review updates or Green/White Paper
developments where relevant.

Note: the SEND and AP Improvement Plan (March 2023) signalled
forthcoming changes. Schools should show awareness of the direction
of travel while complying with current statutory requirements.

## Review Frequency

Annually. The SEND Code of Practice requires schools to publish this
information on their website and update it annually. The governing
body must approve the SEND Information Report.

## Required Content

The SEND Code of Practice (6.79) specifies 14 areas that MUST be covered:

1. The kinds of SEND that are provided for at the school
2. Policies for identifying children and young people with SEND and
   assessing their needs, including the name and contact details of
   the SENCO (required by SEND Regs 2014, Reg 51(a))
3. The school's arrangements for consulting with parents of children
   with SEND about, and involving them in, their child's education
4. The school's arrangements for consulting with young people with SEND
   about, and involving them in, their education
5. Any arrangements made by the governing body relating to the treatment
   of complaints from parents about the provision for SEND
6. How the governing body involves other bodies including health and
   social care, local authority support services, and voluntary
   sector organisations in meeting the needs of children with SEND
7. Contact details of support services for parents of children with SEND
8. The school's arrangements for supporting children with SEND in
   transferring between phases of education or in preparation for
   adulthood and independent living
9. Information on where the local authority's local offer is published
10. The school's approach to teaching children with SEND
11. How the school adapts the curriculum and learning environment for
    children with SEND
12. Additional support available for learning — interventions, specialist
    resources, staffing
13. How the school enables pupils with SEND to engage in activities
    available to others (including physical activities, school trips,
    extra-curricular)
14. Support for improving emotional and social development — including
    measures to prevent bullying

## Quality Indicators

- SENCO named with qualifications (National Award for SEN Coordination)
- Direct contact details for SENCO (phone/email, not just "contact the office")
- Provision map or graduated approach diagram
- Links to local offer
- Parent/carer feedback or voice evident in the report
- Specific data on numbers of SEND pupils (by need type)
- Named governor with SEND responsibility
- Links to related policies (accessibility, equality, behaviour, complaints)
- Easy to read — written for parents, not professionals
- Available in alternative formats on request
- Information about SEND funding and how it is used
- Specific interventions and programmes named
- Staff training and expertise described

## Red Flags

- No SEN information report found on the website
- SENCO not named
- No contact details for the SENCO
- Report appears to be a generic template not customised for the school
- No reference to the local offer
- Report is significantly outdated (2+ academic years)
- Missing more than 3 of the 14 statutory content areas
- No information on how parents can raise concerns
- No mention of the graduated approach (assess, plan, do, review)
- Report is password-protected or behind a login
- Confuses the SEN Information Report with the SEND Policy (they are
  different documents — the policy sets out the school's approach,
  the report provides specific information for parents)

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- All 14 statutory content areas covered
- SENCO named with contact details
- Current academic year
- Link to local offer included
- Specific to the school (not a generic template)
- Approved by governing body

### Partial (40-79)

- Report exists but missing 3-5 statutory content areas
- SENCO named but no contact details
- Appears outdated (previous academic year)
- Template partially customised
- Local offer link missing
- Vague on interventions and support

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No SEN information report found
- Report is 2+ years old
- Generic template with no school-specific content
- Missing more than 5 of the 14 statutory areas
- SENCO not named

## Notes

- The SEN Information Report and the SEND Policy are different documents.
  Both should be published. The Report is the parent-facing document
  that provides specific information; the Policy is the staff-facing
  document setting out procedures.
- Many schools publish the SEND Information Report as a page on their
  website rather than a downloadable document. This is good practice
  as it makes the information more accessible.
- The SENCO should hold the National Award for SEN Coordination (or be
  working towards it). This qualification should ideally be mentioned.
- Schools should link to their Local Authority's Local Offer — this is
  a statutory requirement. The Local Offer provides information on
  services available for children and young people with SEND in the area.
- For academies in a MAT, the SEND policy may be trust-wide but the
  SEN Information Report must be school-specific.
