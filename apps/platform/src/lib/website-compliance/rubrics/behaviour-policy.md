# Behaviour Policy

## Legislation

- School Information (England) Regulations 2008, Schedule 4
- Education and Inspections Act 2006, Section 89
- Behaviour in Schools guidance (DfE, September 2022)
- Equality Act 2010 (reasonable adjustments for SEND pupils)
- KCSIE 2025 (child-on-child abuse link)

## Current References

Must align with the DfE "Behaviour in Schools" guidance (September 2022).
Should reference the school's approach to child-on-child abuse per KCSIE 2025.
Schools may call this a "Behaviour Policy", "Relationships Policy", or
"Behaviour and Relationships Policy" — all are acceptable.

## Review Frequency

Annually. Should be reviewed with staff input. Some schools review
termly in response to behaviour data.

## Required Content

1. The school's approach to behaviour management — vision and principles
2. Clear expectations for pupil conduct (rules, code of conduct)
3. Rewards and recognition system
4. Sanctions and consequences — graduated response
5. Use of exclusion (fixed-term and permanent) — when and how
6. Support for pupils with SEND — reasonable adjustments to behaviour policy
7. Bullying: definition, prevention strategies, and how incidents are dealt with
8. Child-on-child abuse — cross-reference to safeguarding policy
9. Searching, screening and confiscation procedures
10. Use of reasonable force / physical restraint (cross-reference acceptable)
11. Pupil transition support (new starters, year group transitions)
12. Staff roles and responsibilities in behaviour management
13. How parents are informed and involved
14. Recording and monitoring of behaviour incidents
15. Mobile phone policy (if not separate — DfE guidance 2024 recommends
    phones banned during the school day)

## Quality Indicators

- Written in language parents can understand (not just professional jargon)
- Positive and restorative approach evident, not just punitive
- Specific to the school (not a generic LA template)
- Links to related policies (anti-bullying, exclusion, SEND, safeguarding)
- Published as HTML summary with downloadable full policy
- Visual/poster version for pupils
- Clear escalation pathway showing who is responsible at each level
- Behaviour data or analysis referenced to show policy is evidence-based

## Red Flags

- No behaviour policy found on the website
- Policy focuses only on sanctions with no positive behaviour approach
- No mention of SEND reasonable adjustments
- No anti-bullying content or cross-reference
- Policy does not mention exclusion procedures
- Unclear or disproportionate sanctions
- No mention of recording or monitoring
- Policy predates DfE "Behaviour in Schools" 2022 guidance
- References "peer-on-peer abuse" without updating to "child-on-child" (KCSIE 2025)

## Rating Descriptors

### Compliant (80-100)

- Policy published and accessible on website
- Covers behaviour expectations, rewards, sanctions, exclusion
- Addresses SEND reasonable adjustments
- Addresses bullying prevention
- References current guidance
- Review date within last 14 months

### Partial (40-79)

- Policy exists but missing 2-4 required elements
- Outdated references (pre-2022 guidance)
- Generic template not customised to school
- No SEND adjustments mentioned

### Not Found / Non-Compliant (0-39)

- No behaviour policy found
- Policy is significantly outdated (3+ years)
- Brief statement only with no substance

## Notes

- Many schools now call this a "Relationships Policy" reflecting a
  restorative approach. Content requirements are the same regardless of title.
- The DfE "Behaviour in Schools" guidance (2022) replaced earlier guidance.
  Schools should show awareness of this document.
- Mobile phone policies became a prominent issue in 2024 with DfE issuing
  specific guidance recommending phones banned during the school day.
- Anti-bullying may be a separate policy or embedded within the behaviour
  policy. Either approach is acceptable but the content must be present.
