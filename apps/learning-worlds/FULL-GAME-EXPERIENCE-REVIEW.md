# Complete Learning Worlds experience review

7 September 2026. Source: David asked, “Full game, and does it match your expectations?”

**The complete games function, but they do not yet meet the original ambition for an exceptional primary-school adventure.** The visual identity, controls and evidence capture provide a useful foundation. The limiting factor is now the quality of the learning interaction and its connection to the world, rather than a shortage of scenery.

## How the full experience feels

**Nile is closest to the original game concept.** Free walking and animated explorers connect the locations. Its sequence varies the action: water gates, sharing 24 baskets between three boats, inference and evidence, chronology, a shadow investigation, brushing an excavation and selecting museum labels. The smaller-number scaffold genuinely returns to the original maths task. The bonus asks for a physical model, typed calculation and explanation before rewarding it.

However, arrival repeatedly replaces the world with a large reading panel. The gates are three prescribed buttons; the shadow model is a slider in a panel; brushing reveals a flat illustration. The maths can be completed by repeatedly pressing “Share one to each boat” and selecting the supplied explanation. These are useful supported activities, but limited opportunities to make and test a plan. The final museum task culminates in a text journal rather than an exhibition visibly assembled by the child.

Current screenshots reviewed: [free exploration](test-results/full-experience/nile/02-exploration.png), [water gates](test-results/full-experience/nile/03-irrigation.png), [cargo sharing](test-results/full-experience/nile/04-cargo.png), [shadow model](test-results/full-experience/nile/06-shadow.png), [excavation](test-results/full-experience/nile/07-archaeology.png), [completed journal](test-results/full-experience/nile/08-journal.png).

**Plot has a coherent mystery, but little investigative freedom.** The period setting and opening letter help the theme. Evidence, chronology and uncertainty are worthwhile history content. Most actions still follow a fixed sequence of reading, inspecting required clues and choosing an answer. The final display promised by the story is not visibly assembled. Its optional return check is inside Grown-ups rather than presented as a child-facing new mission.

**Fire has strong historical staging and a clearer building action, but remains a guided lesson.** The cutaway house makes placement visible. The street comparison supplies two finished layouts rather than allowing the child to predict and test their own change. Its final and return recall reuse the same questions. The reward leads towards an adult casebook, with little personal payoff for the child.

## What would bring it closer to the brief

1. Connect a meaningful decision to an observable world change. Start with one Fire street investigation: predict, place a gap, compare an illustrative outcome, revise an explanation and try an unfamiliar layout. Keep the model's historical limits clear.
2. Separate teaching with help from an independent check. Preserve mistakes, revisions and support in the record; introduce a fresh example without leaving the answer alongside it. Current records demonstrate practice and completion, not secure mastery or long-term retention.
3. Give each journey a child-owned ending: an assembled exhibition, evidence account or rebuilt street reflecting their actions, plus an appealing return activity. More repeated gems alone will not provide this.

The art has a recognisable, coherent stylised direction, but the screenshots do not meet the earlier request for realistic, state-of-the-art environments and character work. Movement is functional; believable movement on actual school devices and whether children enjoy returning remain unproven. Improve one complete interaction and ending before copying the pattern or adding a fourth world.

Confidence: high in the tested functional results and observed task structure; medium in design prioritisation. Real pupils and a teacher must still assess comprehension, independence, engagement and physical-device usability. This review makes no new claim of curriculum completeness, EEF endorsement or proven educational impact.
